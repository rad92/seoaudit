Below, we compare features, pricing, ratings and support.

Quick verdict: which should you choose?

Choose UpKeep if:

  • you need technicians logging work within days, without an administrator configuring the system first

What are eMaint and UpKeep?

What is eMaint?

eMaint is a cloud CMMS founded in 1986 in Marlton, New Jersey, and part of Fluke Reliability since Fluke acquired it in 2016. It is known for deep configurability and is used by mid-size and large teams in manufacturing, energy, utilities and facilities. eMaint connects natively to Fluke's wireless condition-monitoring sensors and Azima's AI vibration analytics. In March 2026 Fluke launched eMaint AI in beta, adding voice work requests and faster access to asset data.

Strengths

  • Deep configuration of fields, forms and automated workflows
  • Native integration with Fluke sensors and Azima AI vibration analytics
  • Broad Enterprise integrations, including SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Power BI and SCADA

Limitations

  • Higher price: $69 Team and $85 Professional, with a 3-user minimum and Professional prepaid annually
  • Steep learning curve; reviewers say an administrator is needed to get value
  • Offline mobile, condition-based PM and purchasing need Professional; the API and SSO need Enterprise

What is UpKeep?

UpKeep was founded in 2015 by Ryan Chan, a process-development engineer who watched a manufacturing plant's maintenance team lose hours to paper work orders. It began as a mobile-first CMMS and now covers maintenance, safety, training (Learn), fleet and condition-monitoring sensors (Edge) in one platform. UpKeep says more than 4,000 teams use it. Its AI assistant, Nova, is included on every plan through monthly credits.

Strengths

  • Nova AI on every plan
  • Safety, Fleet, Learn and Edge sensors built in
  • Mobile-first app technicians adopt quickly

Limitations

  • No free plan
  • PM starts on Premium, and offline mode on Professional
  • Purchase orders, API access and SSO need Enterprise

eMaint vs UpKeep: feature-by-feature breakdown

Work order management

eMaint: Every plan covers work orders and recurring work orders, with highly configurable fields and forms so each team can track what it needs. Professional adds an automated workflow engine and advanced procedures with inspection checks. Requesters can submit work by email or a web form without a paid license.

UpKeep: Every plan includes unlimited work orders and locations, data importing and unlimited requesters. Technicians create work orders by voice with Voice Fill, and Nova drafts closeout notes and activity summaries. Checklists and time and cost tracking start on Premium, and custom statuses and workflow automation on Enterprise.

The difference: eMaint suits teams that want to configure every field; UpKeep suits teams that want technicians logging work on day one. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep work order created with Voice Fill on a mobile phone]

Preventive maintenance

eMaint: PM scheduling and meter readings are on the Team plan at $69/user/month. Condition-based maintenance triggers need Professional at $85, prepaid annually. eMaint can trigger work from Fluke condition-monitoring sensors, which suits plants already using Fluke hardware.

UpKeep: PM scheduling starts on Premium at $55/user/month. It covers time-based and meter-based schedules and custom checklists, with the Smart Checklist Builder writing checklists from a short description. Edge sensors can open a work order automatically when a reading leaves its range.

The difference: UpKeep gets you PMs and sensor triggers at a lower price per user; eMaint pairs best with existing Fluke sensors. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep PM schedule triggered by a meter reading]

Asset management

eMaint: eMaint supports unlimited assets on every plan, with configurable hierarchies, interactive image mapping that places assets on floor plans or drawings, downtime tracking and QR and barcode scanning. Its configurability lets reliability teams record the asset data their program needs.

UpKeep: UpKeep supports a four-level asset hierarchy, barcode scanning and meter readings from Premium, and asset lifecycle tracking on Professional. Enterprise adds depreciation, warranty and downtime tracking, custom asset statuses and check-in/check-out for shared equipment.

The difference: Both handle complex assets; eMaint is more configurable, UpKeep simpler to set up. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep asset hierarchy showing site, line, machine and component]

Parts and inventory

eMaint: Team includes single-location parts inventory. Professional adds multi-location inventory, a parts forecaster, cycle counting, and purchasing, requisitioning and approvals. Enterprise adds global inventory management across sites.

UpKeep: Parts and inventory management with costing starts on Premium at $55/user/month, with multiple inventory lines per part. Photo-to-Parts creates part records from a photo. Purchase orders sit on Enterprise.

The difference: eMaint puts purchasing on a lower tier; UpKeep gets you inventory with costing for less per user. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep parts inventory with stock levels and reorder points]

Reporting and analytics

eMaint: Standard reports and dashboards are on every plan, and Professional adds advanced reporting and analytics. Reviewers praise how much data eMaint can capture, though several say building custom reports takes effort and training.

UpKeep: Drill-down reporting starts on Premium (30 days of history), with full history from Professional. Enterprise adds custom dashboards, asset downtime and equipment reliability reports, and multi-site modules. Nova can answer questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

The difference: eMaint has more reporting on lower tiers; UpKeep makes data easier to query without building reports. Winner: eMaint.

[Image: UpKeep reliability dashboard with downtime by asset]

Mobile app and offline use

eMaint: eMaint has iOS and Android apps for work orders, scanning and inspections. Offline work mode is on Professional and above. Capterra reviewers rate its mobile experience lower than its other features (3.9 out of 5).

UpKeep: UpKeep started as a mobile app, and its iOS and Android apps cover work orders, scanning, photos, signatures and voice entry. Offline work orders start on Professional, with bookmarked and auto-cached work orders and offline drafts.

The difference: Both gate offline mode to Professional; UpKeep's app is built for technicians first. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep mobile work order with offline draft saved]

AI features

eMaint: Fluke launched eMaint AI in beta in March 2026. It speeds access to asset data, turns documentation into procedures, and creates work requests from voice messages. Separately, Azima's AI vibration analytics feed recommendations into eMaint work orders.

UpKeep: Every plan includes Nova with monthly credits (250 on Essential up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your data, drafts closeout notes, cleans up data and builds checklists. The Nova App Builder creates custom apps from a description, starting on Premium.

The difference: UpKeep's AI is generally available on every plan; eMaint's is newer and in beta. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Nova answering a question about asset downtime]

Integrations

eMaint: eMaint's Enterprise plan includes a RESTful web API and integration hub that connect to SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Power BI, SCADA, PLC and BMS systems, plus 1,000+ apps. Team and Professional don't include the API, so integration needs mean Enterprise.

UpKeep: API access, business integrations and custom integrations are on Enterprise. UpKeep's own Edge sensors connect natively without an integration project.

The difference: Both put integrations on Enterprise; eMaint lists a broader set of enterprise connectors. Winner: eMaint.

[Image: UpKeep integrations settings page]

Safety, fleet and training

eMaint: eMaint supports compliance through audit trails, e-signatures and inspection checks, and offers eMaint University for product training. Fleet maintenance runs as general CMMS assets, without a dedicated DVIR or telematics module.

UpKeep: UpKeep sells Safety (incident capture with OSHA 300, 300A and 301 recordkeeping), Fleet (DVIRs, VIN lookup, telematics) and Learn (courses and certifications) as modules on the same platform and login as maintenance.

The difference: One vendor for maintenance, EHS, fleet and training means fewer tools and invoices. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Safety incident report with OSHA 300 log]

Sensors and condition monitoring

eMaint: As a Fluke company, eMaint connects natively to Fluke's wireless condition-monitoring sensors, and to Azima's vibration analysis services, whose recommendations flow straight into work orders. For plants already on Fluke hardware, this is the strongest reason to choose eMaint.

UpKeep: UpKeep Edge is UpKeep's own wireless sensor line for vibration, temperature and other readings. A reading outside the range you set opens a work order on that asset automatically, and dashboards show sensor trends next to maintenance history.

The difference: eMaint is backed by Fluke's sensor and analysis expertise; UpKeep's Edge is simpler and sold with the CMMS. Winner: eMaint.

Ease of use and implementation

Every new UpKeep customer gets a dedicated implementation and customer success contact through UpKeep's Pledge program, not just a free trial. Teams that want help moving their data can add professional implementation, which covers data mapping, cleaning and formatting, and migration of asset records, inventory and work order history. Training packages range from on-demand training to expanded sessions, and every plan includes the help center, live webinars and a video library. Teams moving from eMaint keep their asset hierarchy and PM schedules; UpKeep's team maps them during migration.

Customer support and onboarding

UpKeep: Standard support on every plan includes 24/7 live chat, phone support 8am–5pm PT on weekdays, and on-demand help-center articles, webinars and videos. Teams that want more can add Premium Support: a priority queue, a dedicated customer success manager, ongoing custom training and consulting, and 24/7 access by chat, text, email and phone. Professional implementation and data migration are available as add-ons.

eMaint: eMaint includes unlimited helpdesk support by phone, email and chat on every plan, plus 24/7 access to online training through eMaint University. Professional adds a dedicated Fluke account manager, and Enterprise a senior account manager. Implementation packages, data cleansing and instructor-led training are sold separately, and customers can attend Fluke's annual Xcelerate conference.

Final verdict: eMaint or UpKeep?

Both are capable CMMS platforms, but they suit different teams. eMaint is built for configuration and Fluke's reliability ecosystem, and it rewards teams with an administrator and an enterprise budget. UpKeep is built for adoption and breadth: lower prices with no minimums, a mobile-first app technicians pick up quickly, AI on every plan, and safety, fleet and training in one system. For most teams, UpKeep is the better fit.

Where eMaint wins: If your plant already runs Fluke sensors or Azima vibration services, and you have an administrator to configure workflows and enterprise integrations, eMaint's Fluke ecosystem is hard to match.

Where UpKeep wins:

  • For teams that want to go live fast, because UpKeep works out of the box without an administrator configuring it first
  • For budget-conscious teams, because UpKeep costs less per user, has no 3-user minimum or forced annual prepayment, and gives free view-only seats
  • For organizations that want maintenance, safety, fleet, training and AI in one platform, because UpKeep builds those in on every plan

eMaint vs UpKeep: FAQs

Is eMaint better than UpKeep?

It depends on your team. eMaint is stronger for plants invested in Fluke sensors and teams with an administrator who wants deep configuration and enterprise integrations. UpKeep is stronger on ease of adoption, price, AI on every plan, and built-in safety, fleet and training. Both rate 4.5 on G2, and UpKeep has over three times as many reviews.

Is eMaint cheaper than UpKeep?

No. eMaint's Team plan is $69/user/month with a 3-user minimum, and Professional is $85, prepaid annually. UpKeep starts at $24/user/month, and its Premium plan with PM and inventory is $55, with free view-only seats. For a 12-person team needing PM and inventory, UpKeep costs about $3,300 a year less.

Does eMaint have a free plan? Does UpKeep?

Neither has a free plan. eMaint offers a free trial after a demo request. UpKeep offers a free trial with no credit card required, and requesters and view-only users are free on every paid UpKeep plan. eMaint requesters can submit work by email or web form without a license.

Which is easier to use, eMaint or UpKeep?

Most teams find UpKeep easier. It started as a mobile app, and technicians can log work by voice or QR scan from day one. eMaint is highly configurable, but reviewers often mention a steep learning curve and the need for an administrator. New UpKeep customers also get a dedicated implementation contact.

Can I migrate my data from eMaint to UpKeep?

Yes. Export your asset hierarchy, PM schedules, parts and work order history from eMaint as CSV or Excel files. UpKeep's team maps your custom fields, cleans and imports the data, and checks every record with you before go-live. Plan the switch around your eMaint renewal date, since its higher plans are prepaid annually.

Do eMaint and UpKeep work offline?

Yes, both do, on their Professional plans and above. eMaint's offline work mode starts on Professional ($85/user/month). UpKeep includes offline work orders on Professional, with bookmarked and cached work orders and offline drafts. Neither offers offline mode on its entry plan.

Which has better reporting and analytics?

eMaint offers more reporting on lower tiers, with standard reports from Team and advanced analytics on Professional, though reviewers say custom reports take effort to build. UpKeep's drill-down reports start on Premium and custom dashboards are on Enterprise, and Nova answers data questions in plain language on every plan.

Which is better for manufacturing or multi-site teams?

For plants already on Fluke sensors and Azima services, eMaint is a strong fit. For manufacturers that want fast technician adoption, OSHA safety recordkeeping, fleet and training in one platform at a lower price, UpKeep is the better fit. Both put multi-site management on their Enterprise plans.

What are the best eMaint alternatives?

The most common eMaint alternatives are UpKeep, Fiix, MaintainX, Limble and IBM Maximo. UpKeep suits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one mobile-first platform. Fiix suits Rockwell plants, MaintainX and Limble simple setups, and Maximo large enterprises.

Who owns eMaint?

Fluke. eMaint was founded in 1986 in Marlton, New Jersey, and acquired by Fluke in 2016. It is part of Fluke Reliability, alongside Fluke's condition-monitoring sensors and Azima vibration services, and Fluke's parent company is Fortive.

Does eMaint have AI features?

Yes, in beta. Fluke launched eMaint AI in March 2026 for faster asset-data lookups, turning documentation into procedures, and voice-activated work requests; Azima's AI vibration analytics also feed eMaint work orders. UpKeep's Nova AI assistant is generally available on every plan, with monthly credits from 250 on Essential to 1,000 on Enterprise.
