EZO vs UpKeep: An Honest 2026 Comparison

Updated October 19, 2026

EZO CMMS is a low-cost maintenance add-on from a company best known for asset and IT inventory tracking, and it suits teams that already track equipment in EZO. UpKeep is a mobile-first CMMS built for maintenance technicians, with AI on every plan and safety, fleet and training in one platform. EZO CMMS starts at $12/user/month (yearly); UpKeep starts at $24. Below, we compare features, pricing, ratings and support.

Quick verdict: which should you choose?

Choose UpKeep if:

  • your technicians work from phones on the floor and need a mobile-first app they'll actually use
  • you want an AI assistant that drafts work orders and answers maintenance questions on every plan
  • you need safety (OSHA recordkeeping), fleet, training or wireless sensors in the same platform as maintenance
  • managers and leadership need reports without paid seats (free view-only users)
  • maintenance, not IT asset tracking, is your main job

Choose EZO if:

  • you already use EZOfficeInventory or AssetSonar and want maintenance in the same ecosystem
  • the lowest per-user price with PM on the entry plan matters most
  • you need purchasing, a REST API or SSO on a mid-tier plan

What are EZO and UpKeep?

What is EZO?

EZO makes cloud asset management software: EZOfficeInventory for asset and inventory tracking, AssetSonar for IT asset management, EZRentOut for rentals, and EZO CMMS for maintenance. EZO CMMS adds work orders, work requests, preventive maintenance, parts inventory and purchasing on top of EZO's asset tracking. In August 2026 EZO launched Zoe, a contextual AI assistant that works across reports, assets, tickets and procurement.

Strengths

  • Low per-user prices, with preventive maintenance on the $12–$14 Essential plan
  • Strong asset tracking: QR and barcode scanning, check-in/out and depreciation
  • Purchasing and a REST API on Plus, and SSO on Professional

Limitations

  • Maintenance grew out of an asset-tracking product; reviewers describe the Planner as desktop-first
  • Reviewers report mobile app glitches and slow bulk operations
  • No safety, fleet, training or first-party sensor modules

What is UpKeep?

UpKeep was founded in 2015 by Ryan Chan, a process-development engineer who watched a manufacturing plant's maintenance team lose hours to paper work orders. It began as a mobile-first CMMS and now covers maintenance, safety, training (Learn), fleet and condition-monitoring sensors (Edge) in one platform. UpKeep says more than 4,000 teams use it. Its AI assistant, Nova, is included on every plan through monthly credits.

Strengths

  • Nova AI on every plan
  • Safety, Fleet, Learn and Edge sensors built in
  • Mobile-first app technicians adopt quickly

Limitations

  • No free plan
  • PM starts on Premium, and offline mode on Professional
  • Purchase orders, API access and SSO need Enterprise

EZO vs UpKeep: feature-by-feature breakdown

Work order management

EZO: EZO CMMS includes unlimited work orders and work requests on its Essential plan, with scheduling in the Planner and QR and barcode scanning to pull up assets. Reviewers find it easy to learn, though some describe the Planner as desktop-first.

UpKeep: Every plan includes unlimited work orders and locations, data importing and unlimited requesters. Technicians create work orders by voice with Voice Fill, and Nova drafts closeout notes and activity summaries. Checklists and time and cost tracking start on Premium, and custom statuses and workflow automation on Enterprise.

The difference: EZO covers work orders cheaply; UpKeep is built for technicians working from their phones. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep work order created with Voice Fill on a mobile phone]

Preventive maintenance

EZO: Preventive maintenance is included from the Essential plan at $12–$14/user/month, with time-based and meter-based schedules. Sensor readings can feed meters through the API, but EZO has no sensors of its own.

UpKeep: PM scheduling starts on Premium at $55/user/month. It covers time-based and meter-based schedules and custom checklists, with the Smart Checklist Builder writing checklists from a short description. Edge sensors can open a work order automatically when a reading leaves its range.

The difference: EZO gets you PM at a lower price; UpKeep adds sensor triggers and AI-drafted procedures. Winner: EZO.

[Image: UpKeep PM schedule triggered by a meter reading]

Asset management

EZO: Asset tracking is EZO's core strength. It supports parent-child assets, QR and barcode tags, check-in and check-out, and depreciation, and the same company sells AssetSonar for IT assets.

UpKeep: UpKeep supports a four-level asset hierarchy, barcode scanning and meter readings from Premium, and asset lifecycle tracking on Professional. Enterprise adds depreciation, warranty and downtime tracking, custom asset statuses and check-in/check-out for shared equipment.

The difference: EZO is stronger for tracking where assets are; UpKeep is stronger for maintaining them. Winner: EZO.

[Image: UpKeep asset hierarchy showing site, line, machine and component]

Parts and inventory

EZO: The Plus plan ($22–$26) adds parts inventory linked to work orders, plus purchasing linked to work orders. That puts purchasing on a lower tier than UpKeep, where purchase orders are on Enterprise.

UpKeep: Parts and inventory management with costing starts on Premium at $55/user/month, with multiple inventory lines per part. Photo-to-Parts creates part records from a photo. Purchase orders sit on Enterprise.

The difference: EZO puts inventory and purchasing on a cheaper tier; UpKeep adds costing and Nova insights. Winner: EZO.

[Image: UpKeep parts inventory with stock levels and reorder points]

Reporting and analytics

EZO: EZO includes pre-built and custom reports on asset usage, maintenance history and costs. Reviewers like the data available but say building custom reports can be complex. Zoe, EZO's AI assistant, can answer questions about reports.

UpKeep: Drill-down reporting starts on Premium (30 days of history), with full history from Professional. Enterprise adds custom dashboards, asset downtime and equipment reliability reports, and multi-site modules. Nova can answer questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

The difference: Both cover standard reporting; UpKeep's Nova answers data questions on every plan. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep reliability dashboard with downtime by asset]

Mobile app and offline use

EZO: EZO's iOS and Android apps support QR and barcode scanning and work orders. Offline, technicians can add work logs to up to 20 work orders and bookmark up to 100 assets. Reviewers report occasional glitches.

UpKeep: UpKeep started as a mobile app, and its iOS and Android apps cover work orders, scanning, photos, signatures and voice entry. Offline work orders start on Professional, with bookmarked and auto-cached work orders and offline drafts.

The difference: UpKeep is mobile-first for technicians; EZO's app grew from asset scanning. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep mobile work order with offline draft saved]

AI features

EZO: In August 2026 EZO launched Zoe, a contextual AI assistant that works across reports, assets, tickets and procurement. It answers utilization questions and generates checklists. EZO has no autonomous agents or AI data audits.

UpKeep: Every plan includes Nova with monthly credits (250 on Essential up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your data, drafts closeout notes, cleans up data and builds checklists. The Nova App Builder creates custom apps from a description, starting on Premium.

The difference: UpKeep's Nova is built for maintenance work; EZO's Zoe is newer and broader across IT and assets. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Nova answering a question about asset downtime]

Integrations

EZO: EZO's Plus plan includes a REST API, and Professional adds SSO and Active Directory. EZO also connects to its own IT asset products, which suits teams that manage IT and facilities together.

UpKeep: API access, business integrations and custom integrations are on Enterprise. UpKeep's own Edge sensors connect natively without an integration project.

The difference: EZO opens its API and SSO on lower tiers; UpKeep lists deeper ERP integrations on Enterprise. Winner: EZO.

[Image: UpKeep integrations settings page]

Safety, fleet and training

EZO: EZO CMMS has no EHS or OSHA module, no fleet module with DVIRs or telematics, and no learning or certification management.

UpKeep: UpKeep sells Safety (incident capture with OSHA 300, 300A and 301 recordkeeping), Fleet (DVIRs, VIN lookup, telematics) and Learn (courses and certifications) as modules on the same platform and login as maintenance.

The difference: One vendor for maintenance, EHS, fleet and training means fewer tools and invoices. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Safety incident report with OSHA 300 log]

Sensors and condition monitoring

EZO: EZO doesn't sell sensors. Sensor or meter readings can be imported through the API to trigger maintenance.

UpKeep: UpKeep Edge is UpKeep's own wireless sensor line for vibration, temperature and other readings. A reading outside the range you set opens a work order on that asset automatically, and dashboards show sensor trends next to maintenance history.

The difference: UpKeep's Edge sensors trigger work orders out of the box; EZO needs API work. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Edge sensor dashboard with temperature alert]

Ease of use and implementation

Every new UpKeep customer gets a dedicated implementation and customer success contact through UpKeep's Pledge program, not just a free trial. Teams that want help moving their data can add professional implementation, which covers data mapping, cleaning and formatting, and migration of asset records, inventory and work order history. Training packages range from on-demand training to expanded sessions, and every plan includes the help center, live webinars and a video library. Teams moving from EZO keep their asset hierarchy and PM schedules; UpKeep's team maps them during migration.

Customer support and onboarding

UpKeep: Standard support on every plan includes 24/7 live chat, phone support 8am–5pm PT on weekdays, and on-demand help-center articles, webinars and videos. Teams that want more can add Premium Support: a priority queue, a dedicated customer success manager, ongoing custom training and consulting, and 24/7 access by chat, text, email and phone. Professional implementation and data migration are available as add-ons.

EZO: EZO offers support by email, phone and chat, a help center and onboarding help, and reviewers often mention same-day responses. Higher plans and Enterprise add more hands-on onboarding and account support.

Final verdict: EZO or UpKeep?

EZO and UpKeep come from different places. EZO is an asset-tracking company that added a low-cost CMMS, and it suits teams that already track equipment or IT assets in EZO. UpKeep was built for maintenance technicians: a mobile-first app, Nova AI on every plan, free view-only seats, and safety, fleet, training and sensors in one system. For teams where maintenance is the main job, UpKeep is the better fit.

Where EZO wins: If you already track IT or office assets in EZO, and you want basic work orders and PM at the lowest per-user price, EZO CMMS is a sensible add-on.

Where UpKeep wins:

  • For teams whose technicians work from their phones, because UpKeep was built mobile-first for maintenance
  • For teams that want AI in daily maintenance, because Nova drafts work orders and answers questions on every plan
  • For organizations that want maintenance, safety, fleet, training and sensors in one platform, because UpKeep builds those in

EZO vs UpKeep: FAQs

Is EZO better than UpKeep?

It depends on your team. EZO CMMS is better for teams that already track assets or IT equipment in EZO and want low-cost work orders and PM. UpKeep is better for maintenance-first teams that want a mobile app technicians adopt, Nova AI on every plan, and safety, fleet and training modules.

Is EZO cheaper than UpKeep?

On list price, yes. EZO CMMS starts at $12/user/month billed yearly, with PM included, and Plus with inventory is $22. UpKeep starts at $24, and PM and inventory start on Premium at $55. UpKeep offsets some of that with free view-only seats and modules EZO doesn't offer.

What is the difference between EZO and EZO CMMS?

EZO is the company and product family. EZOfficeInventory tracks assets and inventory, AssetSonar manages IT assets, EZRentOut handles rentals, and EZO CMMS adds maintenance: work orders, PM, parts and purchasing. Each is a separate subscription.

Does EZO have a free plan? Does UpKeep?

Neither has a free plan. EZO offers a 15-day free trial. UpKeep offers a free trial with no credit card required, and requesters and view-only users are free on every paid UpKeep plan.

Which is easier for technicians to use, EZO or UpKeep?

Most maintenance teams find UpKeep easier in the field. It started as a mobile app, and technicians can log work by voice or QR scan. EZO is easy to learn for asset tracking, but reviewers describe its Planner as desktop-first and report occasional mobile glitches.

Can I migrate my data from EZO to UpKeep?

Yes. Export your assets, PM schedules, work order history, parts and vendors from EZO as CSV files. UpKeep's team maps and imports the data and checks every record with you before go-live. If you use EZO for IT assets, you can keep it for that.

Does EZO have AI features?

Yes. EZO launched Zoe in August 2026, a contextual AI assistant that works across reports, assets, tickets and procurement. UpKeep's Nova is built for maintenance: it drafts work orders, surfaces recurring failures and audits asset data, and it is included on every plan.

Does EZO work offline?

Partly. EZO's mobile app lets technicians add work logs to up to 20 work orders and bookmark up to 100 assets offline, then syncs when they reconnect. UpKeep includes offline work orders on Professional and above.

What are the best EZO alternatives?

The most common EZO CMMS alternatives are UpKeep, MaintainX, Limble, Fiix and Coast. UpKeep suits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one mobile-first platform. Asset Panda and Cheqroom are alternatives for pure asset tracking.
