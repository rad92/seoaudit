Limble vs UpKeep: An Honest 2026 Comparison

Updated October 19, 2026

Limble and UpKeep are both mobile-first CMMS platforms for small and mid-size maintenance teams. Limble is highly rated and puts PM on its entry plan. UpKeep costs less at the tiers most teams compare, gives managers free view-only seats, and adds Nova AI on every plan plus safety, fleet and training modules. Limble starts at about $28/user/month; UpKeep starts at $24. Below, we compare features, pricing, ratings and support.

Quick verdict: which should you choose?

Choose UpKeep if:

  • you want PM and inventory at a lower price per user ($55 vs about $69)
  • managers and leadership need dashboards and reports without paid seats
  • you want an AI assistant that drafts work orders and answers questions on every plan, not just Enterprise
  • you need safety (OSHA recordkeeping), fleet or training in the same platform, without Samsara or extra tools
  • you want first-party wireless sensors that trigger work orders

  • you're a single-site team that wants PM and custom dashboards on the entry plan

What are Limble and UpKeep?

What is Limble?

Limble is a cloud CMMS founded in 2015 and based in Utah, used by small and mid-size maintenance teams in manufacturing, facilities, food and beverage and other industries. It is known for an easy mobile app, fast onboarding and very high user ratings. Its AI features include an AI PM Builder, AI scheduling, Asset Snap, forecasting and anomaly detection, with the newest features on Enterprise.

Strengths

  • Very high ratings (4.8 on G2 and Capterra) and fast onboarding
  • PM, custom dashboards, unlimited free requesters and AI PM Builder on the Standard plan
  • Purchase orders, meter-based PMs and a REST API on Premium+

Limitations

  • Inventory, offline mobile and API need Premium+ (about $69/user/month)
  • Multi-location, custom roles, approval workflows, SSO and ERP/IoT integrations need Enterprise
  • Fleet relies on a Samsara integration and sensors on third parties; managers who view reports need paid seats

What is UpKeep?

UpKeep was founded in 2015 by Ryan Chan, a process-development engineer who watched a manufacturing plant's maintenance team lose hours to paper work orders. It began as a mobile-first CMMS and now covers maintenance, safety, training (Learn), fleet and condition-monitoring sensors (Edge) in one platform. UpKeep says more than 4,000 teams use it. Its AI assistant, Nova, is included on every plan through monthly credits.

Strengths

  • Nova AI on every plan
  • Safety, Fleet, Learn and Edge sensors built in
  • Mobile-first app technicians adopt quickly

Limitations

  • No free plan
  • PM starts on Premium, and offline mode on Professional
  • Purchase orders, API access and SSO need Enterprise

Limble vs UpKeep: feature-by-feature breakdown

Work order management

Limble: Limble's Standard plan includes unlimited work orders, assets and PMs, unlimited free work requesters, QR codes and the mobile app. Reviewers praise how quickly technicians pick it up. Some note that a single work order can't cover multiple assets.

UpKeep: Every plan includes unlimited work orders and locations, data importing and unlimited requesters. Technicians create work orders by voice with Voice Fill, and Nova drafts closeout notes and activity summaries. Checklists and time and cost tracking start on Premium, and custom statuses and workflow automation on Enterprise.

The difference: Both are easy for technicians; UpKeep adds AI drafting and free view-only seats. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep work order created with Voice Fill on a mobile phone]

Preventive maintenance

Limble: PM is included on Standard at about $28/user/month, with Limble's AI PM Builder to draft schedules. Meter and threshold-based PMs need Premium+, and IoT-triggered PMs need Enterprise integrations.

UpKeep: PM scheduling starts on Premium at $55/user/month. It covers time-based and meter-based schedules and custom checklists, with the Smart Checklist Builder writing checklists from a short description. Edge sensors can open a work order automatically when a reading leaves its range.

The difference: Limble puts PM on its entry plan; UpKeep adds sensor triggers with its own Edge hardware. Winner: Limble.

[Image: UpKeep PM schedule triggered by a meter reading]

Asset management

Limble: Limble supports unlimited assets with parent-child hierarchies, QR codes, and asset cost and downtime tracking. Asset Snap uses AI to capture asset details from a photo.

UpKeep: UpKeep supports a four-level asset hierarchy, barcode scanning and meter readings from Premium, and asset lifecycle tracking on Professional. Enterprise adds depreciation, warranty and downtime tracking, custom asset statuses and check-in/check-out for shared equipment.

The difference: Both handle asset hierarchies well; UpKeep adds lifecycle tracking on Professional. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep asset hierarchy showing site, line, machine and component]

Parts and inventory

Limble: Spare parts and inventory, vendors and purchase orders are on Premium+ at about $69/user/month. Cycle counts, multi-location transfers and bulk reordering are on Enterprise.

UpKeep: Parts and inventory management with costing starts on Premium at $55/user/month, with multiple inventory lines per part. Photo-to-Parts creates part records from a photo. Purchase orders sit on Enterprise.

The difference: UpKeep gets you inventory for less ($55 vs ~$69); Limble includes POs at that tier. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep parts inventory with stock levels and reorder points]

Reporting and analytics

Limble: Custom dashboards start on Standard, and Premium+ adds unlimited advanced analytics. Some reviewers say basic reports, such as lists of open work orders, are harder to build than expected.

UpKeep: Drill-down reporting starts on Premium (30 days of history), with full history from Professional. Enterprise adds custom dashboards, asset downtime and equipment reliability reports, and multi-site modules. Nova can answer questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

The difference: Limble shows dashboards on a lower tier; UpKeep lets managers see reports on free seats. Winner: Limble.

[Image: UpKeep reliability dashboard with downtime by asset]

Mobile app and offline use

Limble: Limble's iOS and Android apps are highly rated for work orders, QR scanning and photos. Offline mode is on Premium+ and covers assigned tasks, data entry and time logging. A few reviewers mention occasional glitches.

UpKeep: UpKeep started as a mobile app, and its iOS and Android apps cover work orders, scanning, photos, signatures and voice entry. Offline work orders start on Professional, with bookmarked and auto-cached work orders and offline drafts.

The difference: Both are mobile-first; offline needs Premium+ on Limble and Professional on UpKeep. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep mobile work order with offline draft saved]

AI features

Limble: Limble's AI includes the AI PM Builder, AI scheduling, Asset Snap, forecasting and anomaly detection. The newest features, such as duplicate request prevention, scheduling suggestions and an MCP integration, are on Enterprise.

UpKeep: Every plan includes Nova with monthly credits (250 on Essential up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your data, drafts closeout notes, cleans up data and builds checklists. The Nova App Builder creates custom apps from a description, starting on Premium.

The difference: UpKeep gives every plan Nova; Limble keeps its newest AI for Enterprise. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Nova answering a question about asset downtime]

Integrations

Limble: Premium+ includes a REST API. ERP and IoT integrations, SSO as an included feature, and custom roles are on Enterprise. Limble integrates with Samsara for fleet data.

UpKeep: API access, business integrations and custom integrations are on Enterprise. UpKeep's own Edge sensors connect natively without an integration project.

The difference: Limble opens its API on a lower tier; both put ERP integrations on Enterprise. Winner: Limble.

[Image: UpKeep integrations settings page]

Safety, fleet and training

Limble: Limble has no EHS or OSHA recordkeeping module; 21 CFR Part 11 compliance is on Enterprise. Fleet data comes through a Samsara integration, and there is no learning or certification module.

UpKeep: UpKeep sells Safety (incident capture with OSHA 300, 300A and 301 recordkeeping), Fleet (DVIRs, VIN lookup, telematics) and Learn (courses and certifications) as modules on the same platform and login as maintenance.

The difference: One vendor for maintenance, EHS, fleet and training means fewer tools and invoices. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Safety incident report with OSHA 300 log]

Sensors and condition monitoring

Limble: Limble doesn't make its own sensors. It connects to third-party IoT sensors through integrations on Enterprise.

UpKeep: UpKeep Edge is UpKeep's own wireless sensor line for vibration, temperature and other readings. A reading outside the range you set opens a work order on that asset automatically, and dashboards show sensor trends next to maintenance history.

The difference: UpKeep's Edge sensors work out of the box; Limble needs third-party hardware and Enterprise. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Edge sensor dashboard with temperature alert]

Ease of use and implementation

Every new UpKeep customer gets a dedicated implementation and customer success contact through UpKeep's Pledge program, not just a free trial. Teams that want help moving their data can add professional implementation, which covers data mapping, cleaning and formatting, and migration of asset records, inventory and work order history. Training packages range from on-demand training to expanded sessions, and every plan includes the help center, live webinars and a video library. Teams moving from Limble keep their asset hierarchy and PM schedules; UpKeep's team maps them during migration.

Customer support and onboarding

UpKeep: Standard support on every plan includes 24/7 live chat, phone support 8am–5pm PT on weekdays, and on-demand help-center articles, webinars and videos. Teams that want more can add Premium Support: a priority queue, a dedicated customer success manager, ongoing custom training and consulting, and 24/7 access by chat, text, email and phone. Professional implementation and data migration are available as add-ons.

Limble: Limble offers support through chat, email and a help center, and reviewers often praise its responsiveness and onboarding. Support levels are tiered by plan: Premium+ adds a customer success manager, and Enterprise adds more hands-on implementation and account support.

Final verdict: Limble or UpKeep?

Limble and UpKeep are close rivals, and both are easy, mobile-first CMMS platforms. Limble is highly rated and strong for single-site teams that only need core maintenance. UpKeep costs less at the tiers most teams compare, gives managers free view-only seats, and adds Nova AI on every plan plus safety, fleet, training and sensors in one platform. For teams that want more than core maintenance, UpKeep is the better value.

Where Limble wins: If you're a single-site team that needs core maintenance, values peer ratings highly, and wants PM and dashboards on the entry plan, Limble is a strong choice.

Where UpKeep wins:

  • For budget-conscious teams, because UpKeep Premium costs less than Limble Premium+ and managers get free view-only seats
  • For teams that want AI in daily work, because Nova is on every UpKeep plan, not reserved for Enterprise
  • For organizations that want maintenance, safety, fleet, training and sensors in one platform, because UpKeep builds those in instead of relying on Samsara and third-party hardware

Limble vs UpKeep: FAQs

Is Limble better than UpKeep?

It depends on your team. Limble is highly rated and a strong fit for single-site teams that need core maintenance with PM on the entry plan. UpKeep costs less at the Premium tier, gives managers free view-only seats, and includes Nova AI on every plan plus safety, fleet, training and sensors.

Is Limble cheaper than UpKeep?

No, at the tiers most teams compare UpKeep costs less. UpKeep Essential is $24/user/month versus about $28 for Limble Standard. For PM and inventory, UpKeep Premium is $55 versus about $69 for Limble Premium+, and UpKeep's view-only users are free. Note that Limble includes PM on Standard.

Does Limble have a free plan? Does UpKeep?

UpKeep has no free plan, but it offers a free trial with no credit card, and requesters and view-only users are free on every paid plan. Limble offers a free trial; its free-plan scope has changed over time, so confirm it with Limble. Limble's work requesters are free on every plan.

Which is easier to use, Limble or UpKeep?

Both are known for ease of use. Limble's users rate it 4.8 on G2, and many praise fast onboarding. UpKeep started as a mobile app, technicians can log work by voice or QR scan from day one, and every new customer gets a dedicated implementation contact.

Can I migrate my data from Limble to UpKeep?

Yes. Export your assets, PM schedules, work order history, parts and vendors from Limble as CSV files. UpKeep's team maps and imports the data and checks every record with you before go-live. Most teams time the switch to the end of their Limble term.

Does Limble have AI features?

Yes. Limble offers an AI PM Builder, AI scheduling, Asset Snap, forecasting and anomaly detection, with its newest features on Enterprise. UpKeep's Nova is on every plan: it drafts work orders, audits data, flags recurring failures and answers questions, with monthly credits from 250 on Essential to 1,000 on Enterprise.

Does Limble work offline?

Yes, on Premium+. Limble's offline mode covers assigned tasks, data entry and time logging. UpKeep includes offline work orders on Professional and above, with bookmarked and cached work orders and offline drafts.

Does Limble have fleet management?

Not natively. Limble connects to Samsara for fleet data. UpKeep Fleet is a built-in module with DVIRs, VIN lookup, mileage-based PMs and telematics integration, so many teams can drop a separate fleet tool.

What are the best Limble alternatives?

The most common Limble alternatives are UpKeep, MaintainX, Fiix, eMaint and Coast. UpKeep suits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one mobile-first platform. MaintainX suits communication-first teams, Fiix Rockwell plants, eMaint Fluke-equipped plants, and Coast very small teams.
