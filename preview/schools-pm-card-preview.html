WHAT CHANGES

What a CMMS changes on a campus

Campus teams are chronically short-staffed and the buildings are old. These are the four things they tell us they are trying to fix.

Teachers and staff report problems by email and in person, and the maintenance team works from memory and a paper list.

Anyone submits from a phone with the building and room attached, and the whole backlog is visible in one place.

Summer is the only window for real work, and planning it depends on remembering what needs doing.

Deferred work sits in the system against the asset, so the summer plan is built from a real list.

Buses are maintained on a separate spreadsheet by a separate person.

Vehicles are maintained on mileage or hours in the same queue as the buildings, by the same team if you want.

The board asks what deferred maintenance costs and the answer is a guess.

Asset condition and repair history support a number rather than an estimate at budget time.

KEY WORKFLOWS

Maintenance workflows across a campus

Buildings, grounds, and transportation are one team’s problem on most campuses. These are the hand-offs that matter.

A teacher reports a problem in a classroom

  1. CMMS The request is submitted from a phone with the building and room attached.
  2. CMMS It joins the same queue as scheduled work so priorities are compared honestly.
  3. Nova Ask which buildings generated the most requests this term.

A bus comes due for service

  1. Fleet Service is scheduled on mileage rather than a date on a wall calendar.
  2. CMMS The work order sits in the same queue as the buildings, not a separate system.
  3. Learn Driver and technician certifications are tracked against the same people.

Summer work needs planning

  1. CMMS Deferred work is already recorded against each asset through the year.
  2. Facilities Condition and cost history show which buildings need the window most.
  3. Nova Ask what deferred work is outstanding by building, and what it would cost.

School maintenance software: common questions

What is school maintenance software?

It is the system a district or campus uses to take in work requests from staff, schedule preventive maintenance on buildings and grounds, and keep the record of what has been done to each asset.

On a campus the defining constraints are a small team, an old building stock, and a summer window that has to be planned from something better than memory.

Can UpKeep manage school buses?

Yes, through Fleet. Buses and grounds vehicles are maintained on mileage or hours, and the work orders sit in the same queue as the buildings.

Driver and technician certifications are tracked in Learn, so an expired credential is visible before it becomes a problem.

Can teachers and staff submit requests without a license?

Yes. Anyone can submit from a phone or by scanning a QR code, with no account and no paid seat, and the building and room come attached.

Does UpKeep help with deferred maintenance reporting?

Yes. Deferred work stays recorded against the asset rather than in someone’s head, and asset condition plus repair history through Capital Planning supports a real number at budget time.

If your district runs a formal facility condition assessment programme with a specialist provider, UpKeep complements it rather than replacing the assessment itself.

How much does school maintenance software cost?

UpKeep is priced per user per month, based on the people creating and closing work orders rather than square footage or student count. Staff submitting requests are free. See the pricing page for current tiers.

Can UpKeep handle multiple schools in a district?

Yes. Assets are organized by location, so cost, open work, and PM compliance can be viewed for one school or across the district using one naming scheme.

How long does implementation take?

Weeks, and summer is the usual window. Most districts start by importing the asset list for one or two buildings, setting PMs on HVAC and life safety, and opening request intake to staff.
