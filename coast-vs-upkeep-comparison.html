Coast vs UpKeep: An Honest 2026 Comparison

Updated October 19, 2026

Coast is a simple, low-cost work order and team chat app with a free-forever plan. It suits small teams that mainly need to assign and track work. UpKeep is a full CMMS with AI on every plan and deeper asset, inventory and reporting tools. It also includes safety, fleet and training modules. Coast's paid plans start at $25/user/month (yearly); UpKeep starts at $24. Below, we compare features, pricing, ratings and support.

Quick verdict: which should you choose?

Choose UpKeep if:

  • you need real asset management: a hierarchy, lifecycle tracking and asset-level reliability reports
  • you manage parts with costing, purchase orders and multiple sites
  • you want an AI assistant that drafts work orders and answers data questions on every plan
  • you need safety (OSHA recordkeeping), fleet or training in the same platform
  • managers and leadership need reports without paid seats (free view-only users)

Choose Coast if:

  • you're a small team that mainly needs to assign, track and chat about work, and a free plan matters
  • you want team scheduling, a time clock and shift swaps in the same app
  • you want implementation and training included on a mid-tier plan (Pro)

What are Coast and UpKeep?

What is Coast?

Coast is a cloud work order and team communication app from One Six Nine Inc., used by more than 10,000 teams in facilities, manufacturing, property management, hospitality and other industries. It combines maintenance work orders, preventive maintenance, assets with QR codes, parts inventory, checklists and vendor management with team chat, scheduling and a time clock. Coast's AI builds workflows from a plain-language description.

Strengths

  • Free-forever plan and low paid prices ($25–$28 Starter)
  • Very easy to set up and use; G2 Spring 2026 ranked it #1 for usability
  • Built-in team chat, scheduling and time clock; Pro includes implementation, setup and training

Limitations

  • Reporting, dashboards, external request forms and downtime tracking need Pro ($65 yearly, $72 monthly)
  • Integrations, SSO and multi-site management need Enterprise; the whole organization must be on one plan
  • No dedicated safety, fleet or learning modules, and lighter asset and inventory depth than a full CMMS

What is UpKeep?

UpKeep was founded in 2015 by Ryan Chan, a process-development engineer who watched a manufacturing plant's maintenance team lose hours to paper work orders. It began as a mobile-first CMMS and now covers maintenance, safety, training (Learn), fleet and condition-monitoring sensors (Edge) in one platform. UpKeep says more than 4,000 teams use it. Its AI assistant, Nova, is included on every plan through monthly credits.

Strengths

  • Nova AI on every plan
  • Safety, Fleet, Learn and Edge sensors built in
  • Mobile-first app technicians adopt quickly

Limitations

  • No free plan
  • PM starts on Premium, and offline mode on Professional
  • Purchase orders, API access and SSO need Enterprise

Coast vs UpKeep: feature-by-feature breakdown

Work order management

Coast: Every Coast plan, including Free, has unlimited work orders and real-time messaging, so teams can assign work and chat about it in one place. Starter adds unlimited history, repeating work orders with procedures, time and cost tracking, and 5 workflow automations. Pro adds full workflow customization, external request forms and automated time tracking.

UpKeep: Every plan includes unlimited work orders and locations, data importing and unlimited requesters. Technicians create work orders by voice with Voice Fill, and Nova drafts closeout notes and activity summaries. Checklists and time and cost tracking start on Premium, and custom statuses and workflow automation on Enterprise.

The difference: Coast is simpler for basic work orders; UpKeep goes deeper on assets, parts and reporting. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep work order created with Voice Fill on a mobile phone]

Preventive maintenance

Coast: Coast handles PM through repeating work orders: 5 on the Free plan and unlimited, with procedures, on Starter at $25/user/month. That makes basic time-based PM cheap. Meter-based and sensor-based triggers are not listed on Coast's pricing page.

UpKeep: PM scheduling starts on Premium at $55/user/month. It covers time-based and meter-based schedules and custom checklists, with the Smart Checklist Builder writing checklists from a short description. Edge sensors can open a work order automatically when a reading leaves its range.

The difference: Coast is cheaper for simple time-based PM; UpKeep adds meter and sensor triggers. Winner: Coast.

[Image: UpKeep PM schedule triggered by a meter reading]

Asset management

Coast: Coast tracks assets with QR codes, documents and history, and its asset features cover warranties, service contracts and acquisition-to-disposal tracking. It does not publish hierarchy depth or asset-level reliability reporting outside Pro and Enterprise.

UpKeep: UpKeep supports a four-level asset hierarchy, barcode scanning and meter readings from Premium, and asset lifecycle tracking on Professional. Enterprise adds depreciation, warranty and downtime tracking, custom asset statuses and check-in/check-out for shared equipment.

The difference: Coast covers the basics; UpKeep is built for asset hierarchies and lifecycle data. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep asset hierarchy showing site, line, machine and component]

Parts and inventory

Coast: Coast includes parts inventory management for tracking stock and parts used on work orders. Purchasing and multi-location inventory are not listed on the pricing page, so check them with Coast if you buy parts at scale.

UpKeep: Parts and inventory management with costing starts on Premium at $55/user/month, with multiple inventory lines per part. Photo-to-Parts creates part records from a photo. Purchase orders sit on Enterprise.

The difference: UpKeep is stronger for costing and purchasing; Coast suits simple stock tracking. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep parts inventory with stock levels and reorder points]

Reporting and analytics

Coast: Coast's free and Starter plans don't include reporting and dashboards. Pro ($65 yearly, $72 monthly) adds reporting analytics, unlimited custom dashboards and automated downtime tracking. Enterprise adds custom reporting and multi-site reporting.

UpKeep: Drill-down reporting starts on Premium (30 days of history), with full history from Professional. Enterprise adds custom dashboards, asset downtime and equipment reliability reports, and multi-site modules. Nova can answer questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

The difference: Coast's Pro has strong dashboards but costs more; UpKeep reports start on Premium at $55. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep reliability dashboard with downtime by asset]

Mobile app and offline use

Coast: Coast is mobile-first and highly rated, with 450+ App Store and 300+ Google Play reviews, and it combines work orders with chat in one app. Offline mode is not listed on its pricing page.

UpKeep: UpKeep started as a mobile app, and its iOS and Android apps cover work orders, scanning, photos, signatures and voice entry. Offline work orders start on Professional, with bookmarked and auto-cached work orders and offline drafts.

The difference: Both are mobile-first; UpKeep confirms offline work orders on Professional. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep mobile work order with offline draft saved]

AI features

Coast: Coast markets AI that builds workflows from a plain-language description, which helps teams set up forms and processes faster. Coast does not list an AI assistant that drafts work orders, answers data questions or audits data.

UpKeep: Every plan includes Nova with monthly credits (250 on Essential up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your data, drafts closeout notes, cleans up data and builds checklists. The Nova App Builder creates custom apps from a description, starting on Premium.

The difference: Coast's AI sets up workflows; UpKeep's Nova works inside daily maintenance. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Nova answering a question about asset downtime]

Integrations

Coast: Coast has an integrations marketplace, and its Enterprise plan includes integrations and SSO. Lower plans rely on CSV and PDF export.

UpKeep: API access, business integrations and custom integrations are on Enterprise. UpKeep's own Edge sensors connect natively without an integration project.

The difference: Both put integrations on Enterprise; UpKeep lists deeper ERP and custom integrations. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep integrations settings page]

Safety, fleet and training

Coast: Coast supports inspections and checklists, and it markets fleet maintenance as an industry use case. It has no dedicated safety (OSHA recordkeeping), fleet (DVIR, telematics) or learning module.

UpKeep: UpKeep sells Safety (incident capture with OSHA 300, 300A and 301 recordkeeping), Fleet (DVIRs, VIN lookup, telematics) and Learn (courses and certifications) as modules on the same platform and login as maintenance.

The difference: One vendor for maintenance, EHS, fleet and training means fewer tools and invoices. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Safety incident report with OSHA 300 log]

Sensors and condition monitoring

Coast: Coast doesn't offer its own sensors or condition-monitoring module.

UpKeep: UpKeep Edge is UpKeep's own wireless sensor line for vibration, temperature and other readings. A reading outside the range you set opens a work order on that asset automatically, and dashboards show sensor trends next to maintenance history.

The difference: UpKeep's Edge sensors trigger work orders automatically; Coast has no equivalent. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Edge sensor dashboard with temperature alert]

Ease of use and implementation

Every new UpKeep customer gets a dedicated implementation and customer success contact through UpKeep's Pledge program, not just a free trial. Teams that want help moving their data can add professional implementation, which covers data mapping, cleaning and formatting, and migration of asset records, inventory and work order history. Training packages range from on-demand training to expanded sessions, and every plan includes the help center, live webinars and a video library. Teams moving from Coast keep their asset hierarchy and PM schedules; UpKeep's team maps them during migration.

Customer support and onboarding

UpKeep: Standard support on every plan includes 24/7 live chat, phone support 8am–5pm PT on weekdays, and on-demand help-center articles, webinars and videos. Teams that want more can add Premium Support: a priority queue, a dedicated customer success manager, ongoing custom training and consulting, and 24/7 access by chat, text, email and phone. Professional implementation and data migration are available as add-ons.

Coast: Coast offers live chat support and a help center on every plan. Higher plans add priority support and a dedicated customer success manager, and Pro includes implementation, setup and training. Enterprise customers can pay by invoice.

Final verdict: Coast or UpKeep?

Coast and UpKeep suit different stages. Coast is a simple, low-cost work order and chat app with a free plan, and it is a good start for small teams. UpKeep is a full CMMS: Nova AI on every plan, real asset and inventory management, reporting from Premium, free view-only seats, and safety, fleet and training in one system. Teams that need more than basic work orders usually outgrow Coast, and UpKeep is built for that next step.

Where Coast wins: If you're a small team that mainly needs to assign work and chat about it, and a free plan or the lowest price matters most, Coast is a sensible choice.

Where UpKeep wins:

  • For teams managing complex assets and parts, because UpKeep has a real asset hierarchy, lifecycle tracking and inventory with costing
  • For teams that need reporting without paying for every manager, because UpKeep's reports start on Premium and view-only seats are free
  • For organizations that want maintenance, safety, fleet, training and AI in one platform, because UpKeep builds those in

Coast vs UpKeep: FAQs

Is Coast better than UpKeep?

It depends on your team. Coast is better for small teams that want simple work orders and team chat at the lowest price, with a free plan. UpKeep is better for teams that need a full CMMS: asset hierarchy, inventory with costing, reporting, Nova AI on every plan, and safety, fleet and training modules.

Is Coast cheaper than UpKeep?

At entry level, yes. Coast has a free plan and Starter at $25/user/month (yearly). But Coast's reporting and dashboards need Pro at $65–$72 per user, and every member is billed. UpKeep Premium at $55 includes PM, inventory and reporting, with free view-only seats.

Does Coast have a free plan? Does UpKeep?

Coast has a free-forever plan with unlimited work orders, 5 repeating work orders and 7-day history. UpKeep has no free plan, but it offers a free trial with no credit card, and requesters and view-only users are free on every paid plan.

Which is easier to use, Coast or UpKeep?

Both are known for ease of use. Coast ranks highly for usability and is very quick to set up for simple work orders and chat. UpKeep started as a mobile app, so technicians can log work by voice or QR scan from day one, and every new customer gets a dedicated implementation contact.

Can I migrate my data from Coast to UpKeep?

Yes. Export your work orders, assets, repeating work orders and parts from Coast as CSV files. UpKeep's team maps and imports the data, builds out your asset hierarchy, and checks every record with you before go-live. Coast's annual plans are non-refundable, so plan the switch around your renewal.

Does Coast have AI features?

Yes. Coast's AI builds workflows from a plain-language description. UpKeep's Nova AI goes further: it drafts work orders, answers questions about your maintenance data and cleans up records, and it is included on every plan with monthly credits from 250 on Essential to 1,000 on Enterprise.

Which has better reporting and analytics?

Coast's reporting analytics and unlimited custom dashboards are strong but need the Pro plan ($65–$72 per user). UpKeep's drill-down reports start on Premium ($55), custom dashboards are on Enterprise, Nova answers data questions in plain language, and managers can view reports on free seats.

Does Coast have team chat and scheduling?

Yes. Coast includes real-time team messaging on every plan, and its Team Scheduling & Chat workflow adds scheduling, a time clock and shift swaps. UpKeep focuses on maintenance, with work order comments and notifications instead of a separate chat app.

What are the best Coast alternatives?

The most common Coast alternatives are UpKeep, MaintainX, Limble, Fiix and eMaint. UpKeep suits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one mobile-first platform. MaintainX and Limble suit simple setups, Fiix suits Rockwell plants, and eMaint suits Fluke-equipped plants.
