## 4. Copy-Ready Additions

> These follow the draft's voice: specific, evidence-led, no invented statistics. Swap the bracketed placeholders for real details.

### 4.1 Go Top-Down or Start With a VP?
> Going straight to the executive works when your offer changes something they personally own, like margin, risk exposure or a board-level initiative, and when you can say so in one sentence. Starting one level down works better when the problem is felt first in a function, or when the executive will want an internal recommendation before agreeing to talk.
>
> If you start with a VP or director, qualify their influence before you invest in them. Ask how decisions like this one usually get made, who else would need to weigh in, and whether they'd bring the topic to the executive or prefer you to. A contact who can describe the approval path, and offers to make an introduction, is a sponsor. A contact who can't is a source of information, not a route.

### 4.2 When to Reach Out: Timing and a Sample Cadence
> Executive calendars are crowded at predictable times. Many teams see better connect rates midweek and late in the day, and worse ones on Monday mornings, Friday afternoons, and in the weeks around quarter-end and fiscal year-end. Treat these as starting hypotheses, not rules. Track connect and response rates by day and hour for your own target accounts, and shift the schedule when the data says so.
>
> A baseline cadence to test (about three weeks):
>
> | Day | Touch | Purpose |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 | Email | State the account context, the business implication and the meeting ask |
> | 2 | Call + voicemail | Test role ownership and timing live. Reference the email. |
> | 4 | LinkedIn connection (no pitch) | Add a familiar name to the inbox |
> | 7 | Email | Add one new piece of evidence (peer example or benchmark) |
> | 9 | Call (assistant if needed) | Confirm ownership or ask for the right route |
> | 14 | Email | A sharper question or a relevant account development |
> | 18 | Call | Final live attempt |
> | 21 | Close-the-loop email | Offer to reconnect at a better time; record the outcome |
>
> Change the cadence based on the response. A redirect moves the sequence to the new contact with the context intact, and a timing objection pauses it.

### 4.3 Scripts and Templates
**Cold email (CFO example)**
> **Subject:** [Company]'s margin target and [workflow]
>
> [First name], your Q[x] earnings call put margin improvement at the top of the agenda for [year]. In companies like [peer type], we often find [manual workflow] adding cost that never shows up as its own line item.
>
> [Peer company / anonymized peer] found [specific, verifiable outcome] after looking at this. I'd value 20 minutes to compare notes on whether the same pattern applies at [Company]. If this sits with operations rather than finance, I'd appreciate a pointer.
>
> [Name], [Title], SalesRoads

**Call opener**
> "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with [Company]. I'll be brief. I'm calling because [verified signal], and in companies like yours that often shows up as [business implication]. I don't know whether that's a priority for you this year. Is it worth two minutes to find out, or is someone else closer to it?"

**Gatekeeper / executive assistant**
> "Hi, I'm [Rep] with [Company]. I'm trying to reach whoever owns [business outcome] at [Company]. I believe that's [Executive], but I'd rather check with you than guess. Is that right, and what's the best way to put a short note in front of them?"

**Voicemail (under 25 seconds)**
> "[Name], [Rep] with [Company]. I'm calling about [verified signal] and its effect on [implication]. I've sent a short note with a peer example. If it's relevant, my number is [number]; if not, a quick reply pointing me to the right person would help. Again, [number]."

**LinkedIn connection note (under 300 characters)**
> "[Name], I've been following [Company]'s [initiative]. I work with [peer type] leaders on [outcome] and share what we learn from those conversations. Happy to connect."

### 4.4 Objection Responses (add to challenge #5)
| Objection | Response approach |
|---|---|
| "We're not interested." | "Understood. Is that because [issue] isn't a priority this year, or because you already have it covered? Either answer helps me not waste your time." |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to. So I send something useful rather than a brochure: is [issue] something you're evaluating now, or more of a later-this-year question?" |
| "We already work with a vendor." | "That makes sense. Most of the teams we talk to do. The conversation is usually about whether [specific gap] is covered. Would comparing approaches for 20 minutes be useful even if you stay put?" |
| "No budget." | "Fair. Many leaders use a first conversation to size the problem before budget is set. Would it help to see what [peer] found before planning season?" |
| "Talk to [someone else]." | "Thank you. That's exactly what I needed. May I mention you suggested I reach out?" *(log the redirect)* |
