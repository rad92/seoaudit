## 2. What to move into the kept page, page by page

The kept page already covers: the three reasons (new market/launch, pipeline demand, underperforming sales), KPN / Belden / Fluke examples, the "where outsourced teams create value" table, the performance-based model, and end-to-end capability. **Don't copy anything that repeats those.**

### 2.1 From `how-sales-outsourcing-in-europe-the-uk-can-increase-sales` (the most valuable for SEO)
This page has **3,669 impressions**, the most of any page in the group. They come from UK and Europe queries. The kept page has no UK/Europe section, so add one, or those rankings won't transfer.

**Add a new H2: "Sales outsourcing in the UK and Europe"** (150–250 words). Build it from:
- **Richard Lane's quote**, word for word. It's unique and adds first-hand expertise:
  > "Language, process, structure – all of those things you can get from an outsourcing organisation."
- **Richard's advice on choosing a partner**, word for word:
  > "Be really clear on what success looks like, and also be realistic. Work with the partner, get to know the partner, see how they operate, go and meet their people and work together to build out what the programme needs to look like."
- New copy for the section: native-language/multilingual SDRs, entering European markets without hiring in-country teams, and why UK/EU buyers expect local relevance. Link the existing KPN example (Italy, France, Germany) in the kept page back to this section.

**Don't move** the Brexit paragraph, the 2019 InsideSales.com / Sales Development Summit context, or "three core services incl. recruitment". All are out of date (durhamlane no longer offers recruitment as a service).

Richard's "three main reasons" quotes (skills gap, other focuses, accelerating a proven blueprint) overlap the kept page's three reasons. Optionally add **one line** from the second reason to "2. Generate demand…", because it's a sharp, quotable point:
> "…they outsource the front-end sales piece so the expensive sales resources they do have are out on the road in front of qualified customers."

### 2.2 From `what-to-expect-when-you-outsource-b2b-sales`
**Add a new H2: "What to expect when you outsource sales"** (about 250 words). Condense these four unique sections from the page:
- **"A strategic partnership"**: it's not a shortcut to instant pipeline. Expect an alignment phase on goals, audience and proposition, and a partner that challenges your assumptions.
- **"Immediate access to specialist SDR capability"**: experience from day one, less ramp time, SDRs focused only on sales development.
- **"Transparency and measurable impact"**: judge outcomes (quality of conversations and opportunities, progression), not volume.
- **"Collaboration, not separation"**: feedback flows both ways, and messaging evolves from real conversations.

**Add an H3 (or callout): "What sales outsourcing won't do on its own"**. This is the most distinctive part of the page, and the kept page has nothing like it. Keep it close to the original:
> Outsourcing your SDR function won't compensate for an unclear value proposition, a poorly defined target market, or a lack of internal follow-up capability. Nor should it be viewed as a short-term experiment focused purely on activity.

Skip "A clear, repeatable sales development process". The kept page's "Access to expertise…" section already covers it.

### 2.3 From `dont-believe-everything-you-hear-about-outsourcing`
**Add a new H2: "Common myths about sales outsourcing"** (about 250 words), with three short myth/truth pairs. Open with the three objections the page quotes, because they mirror what buyers search:
1. *"What's the point in paying for B2B sales services when none of the leads actually convert?"* → Truth: cheap per-lead models reward volume. Quality-focused, targeted providers win on conversion rate and deal value.
2. *"How can an external team sell my product when my own salespeople are struggling?"* → Truth: experienced outsourced teams ramp fast on niche and high-value offers, and industry-relevant prospecting is what buyers respond to.
3. *"Why pay for outsourcing when I can hire my own team for the same cost?"* → Truth: there are hidden in-house costs (below), and you get full transparency through reporting.

**Move the in-house challenges list.** It's unique and practical. Use it inside myth 3:
- New starters underperforming for the first 3–6 months while you pay their salaries
- Managers spending time on new starters instead of revenue-generating work
- Lost time and cost when a new starter leaves within the first six months

Link it to the SDR cost calculator and the true-cost-of-SDR-teams post.

**Also worth keeping, as one sentence:** outsourced teams double as market research ("there's no better way to test your proposition than to actually sell it to people").

**Don't move:**
- The crude lines ("mountains of s\*\*t", "weirdos filling out surveys").
- The HubSpot / Time Doctor stats (50%, 69%, 66%). They're 2020-era and link to old sources. Use them only if you find current figures.
- "We know what we're talking about". It's filler.

### 2.4 From `the-true-cost-of-managing-a-sales-operation-and-why-you-should-consider-outsourcing`
**Add an H3 inside the myths section, or under "3. Address underperforming sales": "The hidden costs of running sales in-house"**. Condense the **four Ds** to one line each:
- **Discipline:** sales needs constant rigour on targets, and underperformance often goes unmanaged.
- **Distractions:** B2B sellers get pulled into account and service issues and stop prospecting.
- **Data:** under-investment in front-line prospecting wastes opportunities that aren't obvious.
- **Development:** sales teams aren't kept skilled and current, so competitors tell a better story.

**Don't move** anything else. The lockdown, GDP 2020, EU trade-deal and "unprecedented pressure" content is out of date.

### 2.5 From `how-to-buy-a-sales-outsourcing-solution-2`

## 4. Why not merge sales-team-outsourcing and sales-process-outsourcing

These two are **the strongest pages in the group**, not the weakest:
- **Recent and deep.** Published Aug–Sep 2026, 2,665 and 3,449 words. That's **2–2.6× the length of the kept page**, with original frameworks: a decision-rights map, a 10-step operating model, a "which parts of the sales process can you outsource" table, an 8-stage breakdown, pricing models, the AI role, a 7-step method, and recent sourced stats (Gartner 2025, Gong 2025, 6sense 2026, GovS 008).
- **Distinct search intent.** GSC already maps specific queries to them:
  - **sales-team-outsourcing:** "sales team outsourcing" (56 impr, pos 10.3), "outsource sales team" (pos 6.1), "outsourcing sales team", "outsourced sales team"
  - **sales-process-outsourcing:** "sales process outsourcing" (30 impr, pos 6.8)
  These are sub-topics of "sales outsourcing", not duplicates of it. They're young pages that are still climbing.
- **Merging would hurt the kept page.** Folding about 6,000 extra words of operational detail into a "why outsource" article creates an unfocused 8,000-word page. A 301 also throws away their exact-match titles and URLs.

**Do this instead (it fixes the cannibalisation without losing them):**
1. Keep both. Make each one's angle unmistakable in its title:
   - "Sales Team Outsourcing: 10 Keys to Making It Work" (already distinct)
   - "Sales Process Outsourcing: Which Stages Should You Outsource?"
2. Make **/blog/sales-outsourcing/** the hub. Add a short "Go deeper" block linking to both, using their exact topics as anchor text.
3. Have both pages link back to the hub with "sales outsourcing" as the anchor. sales-process-outsourcing already links to it in its first sentence. Add the same link to sales-team-outsourcing.
4. Make sure neither page competes for the hub's head term. Neither should use "sales outsourcing" alone as its H1.

**If you still decide to merge them,** the pieces worth carrying over are:
- **From sales-team-outsourcing:**
  - the decision-rights map table (client / joint / provider)
  - the 10 keys as a condensed checklist
  - the separated-metrics scorecard (activity → quality → acceptance → progression → pipeline → revenue → ROI)
  - the handoff definition ("A booked meeting records an event. A sales-ready handoff carries enough evidence…")
- **From sales-process-outsourcing:**
  - the "which parts of the sales process can you outsource" table (activity × suitability × human judgement)
  - the 7-step "most effective way to outsource sales"
  - the pricing-models list
  - the AI vs human split
  - the Siemens Healthineers, Konica Minolta and PwC examples
