Updated October 15, 2026

Fiix, part of Rockwell Automation, is a strong pick for manufacturers that want deep reliability reporting, condition-based PMs and links to plant and ERP systems. UpKeep fits teams that want technicians productive fast, AI on every plan, and safety, fleet and training in one platform. Fiix's paid plans start at $45/user/month and UpKeep's at $24. Below, we compare features, pricing, ratings and support.

Quick verdict: which should you choose?

Choose UpKeep if:

  • you need technicians logging work within days, from a phone, without heavy training
  • you want an AI assistant on every plan instead of on a $75 tier or as a paid add-on
  • you need safety (OSHA recordkeeping), fleet and training in the same platform as maintenance

What are Fiix and UpKeep?

What is Fiix?

Fiix is a cloud CMMS founded in Toronto in 2008 and acquired by Rockwell Automation in 2020. It is built for asset-intensive and manufacturing operations, with strong preventive maintenance, reliability reporting and integrations with Rockwell's automation systems. Fiix Foresight adds AI insights on its Professional plan, and Fiix MAX is an AI assistant sold as an add-on. In 2026 Fiix became an official SAP Build Partner, and Rockwell partnered with Augury on predictive maintenance.

Strengths

  • PM triggers by time, meter, condition and alarm, plus nested PMs
  • 100+ prebuilt reports and custom dashboards
  • Integrations with Rockwell systems, PLCs and SAP

Limitations

  • Higher per-user prices: $45 Basic, $75 Professional
  • AI insights and multi-site management need Professional, and the Fiix MAX assistant costs extra
  • Often described as planner-friendly but less intuitive for field technicians

What is UpKeep?

UpKeep was founded in 2015 by Ryan Chan, a process-development engineer who watched a manufacturing plant's maintenance team lose hours to paper work orders. It began as a mobile-first CMMS and now covers maintenance, safety, training (Learn), fleet and condition-monitoring sensors (Edge) in one platform. UpKeep says more than 4,000 teams use it. Its AI assistant, Nova, is included on every plan through monthly credits.

Strengths

  • Nova AI on every plan
  • Safety, Fleet, Learn and Edge sensors built in
  • Mobile-first app technicians adopt quickly

Limitations

  • No free plan
  • PM starts on Premium, and offline mode on Professional
  • Purchase orders, API access and SSO need Enterprise

Fiix vs UpKeep: feature-by-feature breakdown

Work order management

Fiix: Every plan, including the free one, covers work orders, asset management and the mobile app, with an unlimited guest request portal for submitting requests. Work orders carry tasks, parts, labor and files, and Basic adds resource-based scheduling so planners can assign work by available hours.

UpKeep: Every plan includes unlimited work orders and locations, data importing and unlimited requesters. Technicians create work orders by voice with Voice Fill, and Nova drafts closeout notes and activity summaries. Checklists and time and cost tracking start on Premium, and custom statuses and workflow automation on Enterprise.

The difference: Fiix suits planners who schedule by capacity; UpKeep suits technicians who need to log work fast from a phone. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep work order created with Voice Fill on a mobile phone]

Preventive maintenance

Fiix: The free plan includes up to 25 active PMs, and Basic at $45/user/month makes them unlimited. PMs can trigger on time, meter readings, conditions or alarms, and nested PMs stop overlapping jobs from duplicating work. Condition-based maintenance sits on Professional.

UpKeep: PM scheduling starts on Premium at $55/user/month. It covers time-based schedules, meter readings and custom checklists, with the Smart Checklist Builder writing checklists from a short description. Edge sensors can open a work order automatically when a reading leaves its range.

The difference: Fiix gives more PM depth at a lower entry price. UpKeep's edge is sensor-triggered PMs on its own hardware. Winner: Fiix.

[Image: UpKeep PM schedule triggered by a meter reading]

Asset management

Fiix: Fiix builds multi-level asset hierarchies with drag and drop, and tracks asset lifecycle and total cost of ownership. Meter logs and Foresight's anomaly detection add reliability history, and Enterprise adds failure codes for root-cause analysis.

UpKeep: UpKeep supports a four-level asset hierarchy, barcode scanning and meter readings from Premium, and asset lifecycle tracking on Professional. Enterprise adds depreciation, warranty and downtime tracking, custom asset statuses and check-in/check-out for shared equipment.

The difference: Reliability engineers get more out of the box with Fiix. UpKeep's asset depth arrives on higher tiers. Winner: Fiix.

[Image: UpKeep asset hierarchy showing site, line, machine and component]

Parts and inventory

Fiix: Inventory tracking is included from the free plan. Professional adds purchasing and RFQs, inventory cycle counts and automated vendor purchase orders, and Foresight's Parts Forecaster predicts stockouts and recommends purchases.

UpKeep: Parts and inventory management with costing starts on Premium at $55/user/month, with multiple inventory lines per part. Photo-to-Parts creates part records from a photo. Purchase orders sit on Enterprise.

The difference: Teams that run purchasing inside the CMMS get it on a lower tier with Fiix. Winner: Fiix.

[Image: UpKeep parts inventory with stock levels and reorder points]

Reporting and analytics

Fiix: Basic includes reports, with 100+ prebuilt reports and custom dashboards available. Fiix Foresight on Professional adds Asset Insights and Work Order Insights that flag patterns in maintenance data.

UpKeep: Drill-down reporting starts on Premium (30 days of history), with full history from Professional. Enterprise adds custom dashboards, asset downtime and equipment reliability reports, and multi-site modules. Nova can answer questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

The difference: Fiix has deeper reporting out of the box. UpKeep lets people ask questions in plain language instead of building reports. Winner: Fiix.

[Image: UpKeep reliability dashboard with downtime by asset]

Mobile app and offline use

Fiix: Fiix has iOS and Android apps with offline sync, so technicians can keep working without signal. Third-party reviews describe it as capable but desktop-first in practice: planners do most of the setup and analysis on the web.

UpKeep: UpKeep started as a mobile app, and its iOS and Android apps cover work orders, scanning, photos, signatures and voice entry. Offline work orders start on Professional, with bookmarked and auto-cached work orders and offline drafts.

The difference: UpKeep is easier on the phone; Fiix includes offline sync on more plans. Winner: Tie.

[Image: UpKeep mobile work order with offline draft saved]

AI features

Fiix: Fiix Foresight, on Professional and above, offers asset insights, work order insights and parts forecasting. Fiix MAX, an AI maintenance assistant, is a paid add-on. Rockwell's 2026 partnership with Augury adds predictive maintenance through Fiix Asset Health.

UpKeep: Every plan includes Nova with monthly credits (250 on Essential up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your data, drafts closeout notes, cleans up data and builds checklists. The Nova App Builder creates custom apps from a description, starting on Premium.

The difference: UpKeep includes AI on every plan; Fiix reserves AI for $75+ plans and add-ons. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Nova answering a question about asset downtime]

Integrations

Fiix: Fiix is owned by Rockwell Automation and connects to Rockwell's plant systems and PLCs. It became an official SAP Build Partner in March 2026, and its no-code Integration Hub on Enterprise links ERP and other business systems.

UpKeep: API access, business integrations and custom integrations are on Enterprise. UpKeep's own Edge sensors connect natively without an integration project.

The difference: For plants on Rockwell or SAP, Fiix's integrations are a real advantage. Winner: Fiix.

[Image: UpKeep integrations settings page]

Safety, fleet and training

Fiix: Fiix handles safety through inspections and pass/fail checks inside work orders, and tracks user certifications on Basic. It does not offer an incident-management, fleet or learning module.

UpKeep: UpKeep sells Safety (incident capture with OSHA 300, 300A and 301 recordkeeping), Fleet (DVIRs, VIN lookup, telematics) and Learn (courses and certifications) as modules on the same platform and login as maintenance.

The difference: One vendor for maintenance, EHS, fleet and training means fewer tools and invoices. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Safety incident report with OSHA 300 log]

Sensors and condition monitoring

Fiix: Fiix connects to third-party sensors and Rockwell plant data to trigger condition-based work, on Professional. The Augury partnership brings Augury's machine-health sensors and AI into Fiix.

UpKeep: UpKeep Edge is UpKeep's own wireless sensor line for vibration, temperature and other readings. A reading outside the range you set opens a work order on that asset automatically, and dashboards show sensor trends next to maintenance history.

The difference: UpKeep supplies sensors and software from one vendor. Fiix relies on partners. Winner: UpKeep.

[Image: UpKeep Edge sensor dashboard with temperature alert]

Ease of use and implementation

Every new UpKeep customer gets a dedicated implementation and customer success contact through UpKeep's Pledge program, not just a free trial. Teams that want help moving their data can add professional implementation, which covers data mapping, cleaning and formatting, and migration of asset records, inventory and work order history. Training packages range from on-demand training to expanded sessions, and every plan includes the help center, live webinars and a video library. Teams moving from Fiix keep their asset hierarchy and PM schedules; UpKeep's team maps them during migration.

Customer support and onboarding

UpKeep: Standard support on every plan includes 24/7 live chat, phone support 8am–5pm PT on weekdays, and on-demand help-center articles, webinars and videos. Teams that want more can add Premium Support: a priority queue, a dedicated customer success manager, ongoing custom training and consulting, and 24/7 access by chat, text, email and phone. Professional implementation and data migration are available as add-ons.

Fiix: Fiix's free plan relies on self-service resources and community help. Basic adds email and phone support, and higher plans add more hands-on help, with a dedicated customer success manager on top tiers. Implementation and professional services are quoted separately. As a Rockwell Automation company, Fiix can also draw on Rockwell's services and distributor network for larger plant deployments.

Final verdict: Fiix or UpKeep?

Both are mature CMMS platforms, and at single-site scale they cost about the same. Fiix is built for plant reliability: deep PM triggers, strong reporting and Rockwell and SAP integration. UpKeep is built for adoption and breadth: a mobile-first app technicians pick up quickly, AI on every plan, and safety, fleet and training in one system. For teams that value those, UpKeep is the better fit.

Where Fiix wins: If you run a manufacturing plant on Rockwell Automation or SAP and reliability engineering drives your maintenance program, Fiix's integrations and reporting depth are hard to beat.

Where UpKeep wins:

  • For teams where technician adoption is the bottleneck, because UpKeep's mobile-first app gets crews logging work within days
  • For organizations that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one platform, because UpKeep builds those modules in
  • For teams that want AI without upgrading, because Nova is included on every UpKeep plan, while Fiix's AI starts at $75/user/month or costs extra

Fiix vs UpKeep: FAQs

Is Fiix better than UpKeep?

It depends on your operation. Fiix is stronger for manufacturing reliability, with deep PM triggers, 100+ reports and integrations with Rockwell Automation and SAP. UpKeep is stronger on technician adoption, AI on every plan, and built-in safety, fleet and training. Ratings are close: Fiix is slightly higher on G2, UpKeep slightly higher on Capterra.

Is Fiix cheaper than UpKeep?

At the entry level, Fiix has a free plan, but its first paid plan is $45/user/month vs $24 for UpKeep Essential. For PM plus inventory on one site, Fiix Basic ($45) and UpKeep Premium ($55, with free view-only seats) cost about the same per team. Fiix's AI and multi-site features need Professional at $75/user/month.

Does Fiix have a free plan? Does UpKeep?

Fiix does; UpKeep doesn't. Fiix's free plan includes work orders, assets, inventory and the mobile app, with up to 25 active PMs. UpKeep has no free plan, but offers a free trial with no credit card required, and requesters and view-only users are free on every paid plan.

Which is easier to use, Fiix or UpKeep?

Most teams find UpKeep easier for technicians. It started as a mobile app, and crews can log work by voice or from a QR scan. Fiix is powerful but more planner- and desktop-oriented, so field technicians usually need more training. New UpKeep customers also get a dedicated implementation contact.

Can I migrate my data from Fiix to UpKeep?

Yes. Export your asset hierarchy, PM schedules, parts and work order history from Fiix as CSV files. UpKeep's team maps, cleans and imports the data, keeps your hierarchy, and checks every record with you before go-live. Professional implementation packages are available if you want UpKeep to handle the whole migration.

Do Fiix and UpKeep work offline?

Yes, both do. Fiix's mobile app syncs offline. UpKeep includes offline work orders on its Professional plan and above, with bookmarked and cached work orders and offline drafts. If your crews often work without signal, check which plan you need with each vendor.

Which has better reporting and analytics?

Fiix has deeper reporting out of the box, with 100+ prebuilt reports from its Basic plan and Foresight AI insights on Professional. UpKeep's drill-down reports start on Premium, and custom dashboards and reliability reports are on Enterprise. UpKeep's Nova also answers questions about your data in plain language on every plan.

Which is better for manufacturing or multi-site teams?

For plants on Rockwell Automation or SAP, Fiix is often the stronger fit thanks to its native integrations and reliability depth. For manufacturers that want fast technician adoption, OSHA safety recordkeeping, fleet and training in one platform, UpKeep is the better fit. Multi-site management is on Fiix Professional and UpKeep Enterprise.

What are the best Fiix alternatives?

The most common Fiix alternatives are UpKeep, MaintainX, Limble, eMaint and IBM Maximo. UpKeep suits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet and training in one mobile-first platform. MaintainX and Limble are known for simple setup, and eMaint and Maximo for enterprise reliability.

Who owns Fiix?

Rockwell Automation. It acquired Fiix, a Toronto-based CMMS company founded in 2008, in late 2020. Fiix is part of Rockwell's software business and integrates with its FactoryTalk automation systems. In 2026 it also became an official SAP Build Partner and partnered with Augury on predictive maintenance.

Does UpKeep have AI features like Fiix Foresight?

Yes. Every UpKeep plan includes Nova, an AI assistant with monthly credits (250 on Essential, up to 1,000 on Enterprise). Nova answers questions about your maintenance data, drafts work order notes, builds checklists and cleans up data. Fiix's Foresight AI starts on its $75 Professional plan, and its Fiix MAX assistant is a paid add-on.
