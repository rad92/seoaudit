The short answer

UpKeep, MaintainX, Fiix, eMaint, Limble, Coast and EZO all handle work orders and preventive maintenance. They differ in price, depth and what's included. MaintainX and Coast suit small teams that want a free plan, Fiix and eMaint suit asset-heavy plants, Limble suits single sites, and EZO suits teams tracking IT assets. UpKeep fits teams that want maintenance, safety, fleet, training and AI in one platform.

How to compare CMMS software

Feature checklists make most CMMS tools look alike. These eight criteria show the differences that matter after go-live.

  1. Technician adoption. Watch a technician close a work order on a phone: by voice, by QR scan, and offline. Check which plan includes offline mode.
  2. The price of the plan you'll actually use. Price the plan with PM and parts inventory, not the entry plan.
  3. Who needs a paid seat. Count managers, requesters and leadership. Free view-only seats can change the total by thousands a year.
  4. What sits on Enterprise. API access, SSO, multi-site management, purchase orders and custom roles often need a custom contract.
  5. Asset depth. Hierarchy levels, meter- and sensor-based PM, and downtime and reliability reporting.
  6. Modules beyond maintenance. Safety and OSHA recordkeeping, fleet and training in one system, or separate tools to buy and connect.
  7. AI that helps daily work. Look for drafting work orders, answering data questions and cleaning records, and check which plan includes it.
  8. Implementation and support. Who migrates your data, how long go-live takes, and what support hours each plan includes.

5 common comparison mistakes

  • Comparing feature lists instead of how your team works each day.
  • Pricing the entry plan, then discovering PM or inventory is a tier up.
  • Ignoring technician adoption: a CMMS nobody uses on the floor fails.
  • Underestimating implementation: data clean-up and PM setup take the most time.
  • Skipping integrations and ecosystems you already rely on, such as Rockwell, Fluke or an ERP.

Questions to ask every vendor

  • Which plan do we need for PM, parts inventory and offline mobile?
  • Which users can be free: requesters, viewers, managers?
  • What costs extra: implementation, SSO, API, sensors, premium support?
  • Is there a minimum seat count or a prepaid annual commitment?
  • Will you migrate our assets, PMs and work order history, and how long does go-live take?

Hidden costs to budget for

  • Implementation and data migration packages
  • Add-ons such as SSO, API access, AI assistants or sensor hardware
  • Minimum seats and prepaid annual terms
  • Separate tools for safety, fleet or training when the CMMS doesn't include them

CMMS comparison FAQs

What is the best way to compare CMMS software?

Compare on workflows, not feature lists: how technicians log work on a phone, what PM and inventory cost per user, which seats are free, and what needs an Enterprise contract. Shortlist 3–5 tools, trial two with your own assets, and price the plan you'll actually need.

Which CMMS is cheapest?

EZO CMMS has the lowest list price ($12/user/month). Coast, MaintainX and Fiix have free plans. Once PM and parts inventory are needed, UpKeep Premium ($55) costs less than MaintainX Premium ($65), Limble Premium+ (~$69) and eMaint Team ($69), and manager seats are free.

Which CMMS has a free plan?

MaintainX (Basic), Fiix (Free, limited users) and Coast (Free) have free-forever plans. UpKeep, eMaint, Limble and EZO offer free trials instead. Free plans cap PMs, history or users, so check the limit that matters to you.

Which CMMS is easiest to use?

MaintainX, Limble and Coast score highest for ease of use on G2. UpKeep is mobile-first, with voice and QR work orders, and every new customer gets an implementation contact. eMaint and Fiix are more configurable but take longer to set up.

Which CMMS is best for manufacturing?

Fiix suits Rockwell plants, and eMaint suits plants on Fluke sensors. UpKeep suits manufacturers that want maintenance, OSHA safety, training and its own Edge sensors in one platform. Limble and MaintainX work well for single-site plants with core needs.

Which CMMS includes AI?

UpKeep includes Nova on every plan, plus Supernova agents. MaintainX keeps its AI assistant on Enterprise, Fiix sells it as an add-on, eMaint's is in beta, Limble puts its newest AI on Enterprise, and EZO launched Zoe in August 2026.

Do CMMS tools work offline?

Most do, on different plans: UpKeep and eMaint from Professional, Limble from Premium+, and MaintainX and Fiix on their main plans. EZO allows limited offline work logs. Check which plan you'll need before you buy.

How much does a CMMS cost per year?

For a 12-person team needing PM and inventory, expect about $3,000–$10,000 a year on list prices: about $3,200 on EZO Plus, $6,600 on UpKeep Premium (with 2 free manager seats), $9,360 on MaintainX Premium and $9,936 on Limble Premium+.

What's the difference between CMMS and EAM?

A CMMS runs day-to-day maintenance: work orders, preventive maintenance, assets and parts. EAM software covers an asset's whole life, from planning a purchase to retiring it, including costs, capital projects and compliance across the enterprise. Most maintenance teams start with a CMMS; large asset-intensive organizations add or choose an EAM.

How long does CMMS implementation take?

Most rollouts take a few weeks to a few months. Timing depends on how much asset, PM and work order data you migrate and how much configuration the tool needs. Simple tools like Coast and MaintainX can start in days, while eMaint and Fiix usually need more setup. UpKeep gives every new customer an implementation contact. There's no single best CMMS, only the best fit. MaintainX and Coast are the easiest free starts for small teams. Limble suits single sites that want top-rated simplicity. Fiix and eMaint suit asset-heavy plants tied to Rockwell or Fluke, and EZO suits teams already tracking IT assets in EZO.
