★Summary

What the page becomes. The refreshed /blog/cmms-comparison/ becomes UpKeep's CMMS comparison hub. Its job is a side-by-side comparison of seven tools: UpKeep, MaintainX, Fiix, eMaint, Limble, Coast and EZO. It is built around a filterable matrix, a pricing and ratings snapshot, a "which tool fits you" picker, and a card for each tool that links to its "vs UpKeep" page. It is a tool for comparing, not a ranked "best of" list. That job stays with /blog/best-cmms-software/.

Why separate the two pages. In Google Search Console (GSC), this page gets 1,505 impressions and 0 clicks for "cmms comparison". It also gets 806 impressions and 0 clicks for "best cmms software", a query /best-cmms-software/ already wins (13 clicks from 2,961 impressions). The overlap splits Google's signal. Giving the page one clear intent (compare, chart, matrix) should lift CTR on the queries it already ranks for: it is #2 for "cmms software comparison".

Top changes, in priority order

  1. Lead with the answer and the matrix. A 60-word verdict, then a seven-tool comparison table above the fold. Today the first table sits below a methodology section.
  2. Add the two tools missing today, Coast and EZO. The vs-page research is done, and both rank on their own comparison pages.
  3. Show prices and ratings in one visual, with a "checked on" date. Accruent's ranking page lists UpKeep at outdated prices (Lite $20, Starter $45, Professional $75), so publishing the current numbers clearly corrects the record.
  4. Replace "What users love" with honest cards. Each card gives best for, starting price, standout, watch-out and a link to the vs page. A credible comparison has to admit where competitors win.
  5. Add a decision picker and a methodology box with a named author and reviewer. These are the E-E-A-T signals the current "UpKeep Staff" byline lacks.
  6. Turn the page into the internal-link hub for the six "vs UpKeep" pages, and link back to /best-cmms-software/ for the ranked list.

2Competitor analysis: how ranking CMMS comparison guides are built

The Google results for "cmms software comparison" and "cmms comparison chart" turned up nine pages besides UpKeep's: Comparesoft (#1 for the main query), TMA Systems, MRI Software, Accruent, OxMaint, Fabrico, Vectra, Maintainly and Limble. Limble's is an enterprise list rather than a comparison, so the chart covers the other eight.

Seven of the eight are vendor pages that put their own product first. Only Comparesoft is neutral, and it is a lead-generation directory. A vendor page that admits where rivals win, shows sourced prices and ratings, and has a named author would be the most credible result on page one.

Patterns to copy

  • The table comes first. Accruent, MRI, Maintainly and Vectra all put the comparison table in the first or second section.
  • Best for beats a score. Pages label each tool by fit ("multi-site", "regulated industries", "small teams") instead of rating it "excellent".
  • Buying criteria and mistakes. TMA's "comparing features instead of workflows" and "ignoring technician adoption" sections are a strong angle for UpKeep, whose case rests on mobile adoption.
  • A hidden-costs section. Accruent and OxMaint both call out implementation, add-ons and annual lock-in.

Where UpKeep can beat them

  • Current, dated, sourced prices for all seven tools. Two ranking pages show wrong UpKeep prices today.
  • G2 and Capterra ratings with review counts. Only one competitor shows any ratings.
  • An interactive picker and filterable matrix. Only Accruent (pricing calculator) and Comparesoft (lead form) have anything interactive.
  • A named author and reviewer, a methodology box and a "last checked" date.
  • Links to a dedicated vs page for every tool, which no competitor offers for all its tools (Vectra comes closest).

4Page architecture

Each stage has its own exit, so a reader can leave with the next step that matches how far along they are. A reader who has already decided books a demo from the verdict. One who has UpKeep on the shortlist starts a trial from the picker. One weighing a single rival goes to that vs page from its tool card. A secondary link to /best-cmms-software/ sits in the intro for readers who want a ranked list instead.

7Pricing and ratings visuals (page sections 2 and 4)

Build both charts on the page as live, accessible SVG or HTML, not as screenshots, so they can be updated when prices change and so Google can read the numbers.

The chart makes UpKeep's real pricing argument: it is mid-pack on the entry plan but cheaper than three rivals once PM and inventory are needed. It also leaves out free view-only seats, which save more as teams add managers. Put the 12-person worked example right under it: 10 technicians plus 2 managers on UpKeep Premium cost $6,600 a year, against $9,360 on MaintainX Premium (12 paid seats) and $9,936 on Limble Premium+.

Show this chart even though UpKeep doesn't top it. Under it, one sentence: rivals with smaller, simpler products tend to score higher on G2, and UpKeep's score rests on one of the largest review bases of the seven (1,099 reviews; confirm MaintainX's count before claiming the most). Link each tool's score to its G2 page.

Schema

  • Article with a named author (Person), a reviewer and dateModified that matches the visible "Last checked" date.
  • FAQPage for the 11 FAQs below.
  • ItemList of the 7 tools, in table order, each linking to its card anchor.
  • BreadcrumbList: keep it.
  • Avoid AggregateRating or Review markup for competitors' products. Google treats it as self-serving, and it can't be sourced from G2 in schema.

E-E-A-T

  • A named author with maintenance or reliability experience, and a named reviewer.
  • A "How we compared" box: vendor pricing pages and G2 and Capterra, checked on [date]; hands-on trials where they happened; "UpKeep makes one of these tools" stated plainly.
  • Every competitor price and claim links to its source.
  • A visible changelog line, for example: "Updated 10 Nov 2026: added Coast and EZO, refreshed prices."

FAQs (FAQPage)

QuestionAnswer direction (40–70 words)
What is the best way to compare CMMS software?Compare on workflows, not feature lists: how technicians log work on a phone, what PM and inventory cost per user, which seats are free, and what needs an Enterprise contract. Shortlist 3–5 tools, trial two with your own assets, and price the plan you'll actually need.
Which CMMS is cheapest?EZO CMMS has the lowest list price ($12/user/month). Coast, MaintainX and Fiix have free plans. Once PM and parts inventory are needed, UpKeep Premium ($55) costs less than MaintainX Premium ($65), Limble Premium+ (~$69) and eMaint Team ($69), and manager seats are free.
Which CMMS has a free plan?MaintainX (Basic), Fiix (Free, limited users) and Coast (Free) have free-forever plans. UpKeep, eMaint, Limble and EZO offer free trials instead. Free plans cap PMs, history or users, so check the limit that matters to you.
Which CMMS is easiest to use?MaintainX, Limble and Coast score highest for ease of use on G2. UpKeep is mobile-first, with voice and QR work orders, and every new customer gets an implementation contact. eMaint and Fiix are more configurable but take longer to set up.
Which CMMS is best for manufacturing?Fiix suits Rockwell plants, and eMaint suits plants on Fluke sensors. UpKeep suits manufacturers that want maintenance, OSHA safety, training and its own Edge sensors in one platform. Limble and MaintainX work well for single-site plants with core needs.
Which CMMS includes AI?UpKeep includes Nova on every plan, plus Supernova agents. MaintainX keeps its AI assistant on Enterprise, Fiix sells it as an add-on, eMaint's is in beta, Limble puts its newest AI on Enterprise, and EZO launched Zoe in August 2026.
Do CMMS tools work offline?Most do, on different plans: UpKeep and eMaint from Professional, Limble from Premium+, and MaintainX and Fiix on their main plans. EZO allows limited offline work logs. Check which plan you'll need before you buy.
How much does a CMMS cost per year?For a 12-person team needing PM and inventory, expect about $3,000–$10,000 a year on list prices: about $3,200 on EZO Plus, $6,600 on UpKeep Premium (with 2 free manager seats), $9,360 on MaintainX Premium and $9,936 on Limble Premium+.
What's the difference between CMMS and EAM?Keep and tighten the current answer: a CMMS runs day-to-day maintenance, while EAM covers the full asset lifecycle, costs and capital planning.
How long does CMMS implementation take?Keep the current answer, adding UpKeep's own go-live figure (to be supplied) and a note that eMaint and Fiix usually need more configuration.
Is UpKeep better than MaintainX, Limble or Fiix?It depends on the team. Link each tool to its vs page with its one-line verdict. This answer feeds the multi-brand queries.
