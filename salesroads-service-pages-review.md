# Service Pages Review: salesroads.com

**Review date:** 2026-11-13
**Pages reviewed (live):** /outsourced-sdr-services/, /appointment-setting-services/, /lead-generation-services/, /outbound-calling-services/, /outbound-email-services/, /list-building-services/, /inbound-appointment-setting/, /account-reactivation/, /account-based-marketing-data/, /sales-intent-data/
**Benchmarked against:** memoryBlue (SDR services), Sales Focus (appointment setting), Callbox (outsourced SDR), Superhuman Prospecting (B2B appointment setting). These pages outrank SalesRoads for the head terms.

## Verdict: 🟠 Not strong enough yet

The service pages look polished, but they're **brochures built from a shared template**, not pages that answer what a buyer (or Google) needs before booking a call. Three problems matter most:

1. **They contradict the pricing page and each other.** The #2 commercial page still shows an old pricing table ($6,950 and $9,500) and sells "fractional SDRs", which /pricing says don't exist.
2. **They're thin where it counts.** The Outsourced SDR page, which should be the pillar for SalesRoads' core product, has **about 450 words of unique copy**. The page that ranks best for that term (memoryBlue) has about 3,100.
3. **They leave out decision content:** real cost, who it's for, results to expect, in-house vs outsourced, how a "qualified" meeting is defined, data ownership and compliance. Competitors cover these, and so does SalesRoads' own /pricing page, but the service pages don't.

The raw material is excellent: transparent pricing, 33 case studies with numbers, a real methodology and named roles. It just isn't on the service pages.

## 2. What's wrong across the board

### 2.1 Facts that contradict /pricing and each other (fix first, because buyers notice)
| Topic | Service pages say | Source of truth |
|---|---|---|
| Price | "Fractional SDR… starts at **$6,950 / 4 weeks**"; "Full SDR… starts at **$9,500 / 4 weeks**" (/appointment-setting-services); "High-quality… starts around **$12,000 per month**" (appointment + SDR FAQs) | /pricing: **$11,950 per 4 weeks** for 1 dedicated SDR, scaling to $43,150 for 6 |
| Rep model | "**fractional SDRs**" (/appointment-setting-services) | /pricing: "100% dedicated rep — never shared across accounts" |
| SDR experience | "5–10 years" (appointment) / "7 years avg." (SDR, executives) | /pricing: "average of **14 years**". Pick one. |
| Years in business | 15+ (appointment meta and schema), 17+ (calling), 19+ (elsewhere) | "Since 2007" doesn't go out of date |
| Team roles | "Director of Client Success", "Talent Development Manager", "TDM's" | /pricing: Client Strategist, SDR Performance Coach, Optimization Specialist, Research & Data Team |
| Optimization Specialist | "An additional SDR deployed to accelerate results" (SDR page) | /pricing: "Reviews metrics week over week and adjusts targeting, talk tracks, and cadence" |
| Email infrastructure | "dedicated IPs per client"; verified "through Unbounce" | /pricing: "multiple sending domains and mailboxes". Confirm the verification tool (probably NeverBounce). |
| Case-study numbers | Bid Retriever "1100 appointments" | Case study: 1,164 |

### 2.2 Too much template, too little page-specific substance
- Every service page carries the same reviews widget (Ron Cohen / Calvin Clark / Bryan Luoma), the same "Ready to Grow? Cancel anytime" block, and the same 6–10 case-study cards (logo + title only, no numbers). Google sees near-duplicate pages, and buyers see the same proof everywhere, so none of it feels specific.
- Copy-paste errors show it: the **email** page's sections are headed "America's Most-Trusted **Appointment Setting** Service" and "award-winning **appointment setting** services."
- "Related blog posts" on the SDR page date from **2021–2022**.

### 2.3 Missing the content buyers decide on
Benchmark: the memoryBlue SDR page (about 3,100 words) covers what outsourced SDR services are, when to outsource, how it works (with ramp time), what's included, **results to expect**, **who it's best for**, **how much it costs** (fully loaded in-house SDR about $140K/yr vs outsourced), an **in-house vs outsourced table**, and 9 FAQs including "Who owns the prospect data?" and "How is performance measured?". Sales Focus and Superhuman Prospecting each run about 1,050–1,850 words, with FAQs, process, rep location and contract terms.

The SalesRoads pages are missing:
- **The price on the page.** SalesRoads publishes it, and most competitors don't. That's the biggest differentiator in the category, and the service pages hide it behind a link (or show the wrong one).
- **What counts as a qualified appointment** (BANT is mentioned only on the homepage), **show rate**, and the replacement policy for no-shows or wrong-fit meetings.
- **Timeline to first meetings / ramp** (only the launch timeline is given).
- **Who it's for / not for** (deal size, ACV, sales cycle). The pricing video says there's a "who it isn't a fit for" answer, but it's not written anywhere.
- **In-house vs outsourced comparison.** The data exists in /outsourcing/in-house-sdr-costs/ and on /pricing.
- **Data ownership, CRM, reporting.** The dashboard is mentioned but there's no sample. The email page shows one; reuse that pattern.
- **Compliance:** TCPA/DNC scrubbing, CAN-SPAM/opt-outs, GDPR/CASL for international calling. None of the pages mention it. For a phone-first company this is a trust signal, and enterprise buyers ask about it.
- **Where reps are based and how they're employed** (US-based, W-2 via PEO, remote). Superhuman Prospecting answers this in its first FAQ.
- **Contract terms** in plain words: 4-week billing, cancel anytime, the recommended 12-week lab.

### 2.4 Proof isn't matched to the service
The site has strong, specific numbers, but they're on case-study pages, not where buyers decide. For example:
- **Calling:** Revel Systems (30,000 businesses called; 3,751 transfers and appointments), Unishippers (cost per appointment 20% below its internal team), Bid Retriever (1,164 appointments, 3.5+ per calling day)
- **Appointment setting:** AchieveIt (937 appointments; $540K ARR from 18 deals), Bold Penguin (7,198 appointments), Factor (26% of appointments closed)
- **Lead gen:** Parker Hannifin (541 SQLs, about $25M a year), Copperweld (2+ opportunities per SDR per day), MARTA (587 partnership opportunities)
- **SDR program:** Clean Energy (1,060+ meetings → 600 demos → 172 opportunities), Black River Group ($28M pipeline, 90% qualified), Layfield ($1.25M pipeline in 15 weeks)
- **List building / data:** none. Ask the client for one data-quality case (bounce rate, connect rate).

### 2.5 On-page and technical
- **Duplicate H1s** on 5 pages (lead gen, calling, email, inbound, reactivation). Heading levels are used for styling (H5/H6 labels, empty H3s on the SDR, ABM and intent pages).
- **Titles:** email, inbound and reactivation still carry the 74-character site-name suffix (audit §1.1). Lead gen's title has no brand. Calling's title says "Cold Calling Outsourcing", but neither the H1 nor the body targets "outsourced cold calling".
- **Schema:** `Article` on service pages; invalid lowercase `product`/`aggregateRating` on appointment setting. Use `Service` (provider → Organization, areaServed, offers from /pricing) + `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList`.
- **Internal links:** every service page links to every other one only through the nav. There are no contextual links between, for example, cold calling and appointment setting, or from industry pages to services.

### 2.6 The architecture doesn't match how SalesRoads sells
/pricing describes **one product** ("One program, one price"): a dedicated SDR program in two flavors (Appointment Setting → SQLs, Market Research Lead Generation → MQLs), with calling, email and data included, plus two add-ons (inbound, reactivation). The site presents **10 parallel "services"**, including channels (calling, email) and data products (list building, ABM data, intent data) that /pricing never prices separately. That confuses buyers ("can I buy just email?"), and it spreads topical authority thin. The SDR and appointment-setting pages target overlapping intent.

**Confirm with the client:** are list building, ABM data, intent data and email-only sold standalone? The answer decides the structure below.

## 4. The service-page template every money page should follow

| # | Module | What goes in it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | One H1 with the primary keyword; a one-sentence outcome promise; price anchor ("Programs from $11,950 per 4 weeks · cancel anytime"); 2 CTAs (Let's Talk, See pricing) | Price in the hero is the differentiator |
| 2 | **Proof bar** | 3 service-specific metric callouts from case studies + Clutch 4.9 (≈67 reviews) / G2 4.9 | Different metrics on each page |
| 3 | **What it is and who it's for** | 120–200 words; ideal fit (B2B, ACV, sales cycle, deal size); **not a fit** (B2C, under $5K/mo budgets, transactional sales) | Answers the definition query too |
| 4 | **What's included** | The 5 roles + deliverables + channels, with the **same names as /pricing** | Pull from /pricing so it can't drift |
| 5 | **How it works** | Kickoff → playbook → list → training → calling day 16 → weekly optimization; **when first meetings typically arrive** | Add ramp expectations |
| 6 | **What a qualified meeting means** | Qualification criteria (BANT or the client's), show-rate tracking, what happens with no-shows and wrong-fit meetings | Biggest trust gap in the category |
| 7 | **Results to expect + case studies** | 2–3 mini case studies with numbers *for this service* | See §2.4 |
| 8 | **Pricing** | Real price table or a short summary + link; billing model; cancel anytime; recommended 12 weeks | Never hard-code different numbers: use one shared block |
| 9 | **In-house vs SalesRoads** | Table: time to launch, fully loaded cost, management load, turnover risk, data/tech, flexibility | Link /outsourcing/in-house-sdr-costs/ |
| 10 | **Reps, compliance and data** | US-based W-2 reps, training, interview your SDRs; TCPA/DNC, CAN-SPAM, GDPR/CASL; client owns the data; works in the client's CRM | |
| 11 | **FAQ (6–10)** | Cost, launch time, contract, data ownership, reporting, rep location, qualification, industries, difference from appointment setting / lead gen | `FAQPage` schema |
| 12 | **Related** | Contextual links to sibling services, 2 industry pages, 2–3 current blog guides (2025–26) | Replace 2021–22 teasers |

Target length: **1,800–2,500 words of unique copy** for the four core pages (SDR, appointment setting, lead gen, cold calling); 1,000–1,500 for channel and add-on pages. Keep the shared reviews widget, but put service-specific proof above it.

## 5. Page-by-page actions

**Outsourced SDR Services (rebuild: largest gap, core product)**
- Title: `Outsourced SDR Services: Dedicated US SDR Teams | SalesRoads` (60). Meta: "Get a dedicated outbound SDR team with strategist, coach, data and dashboard, live in 15 business days. From $11,950 per 4 weeks. Cancel anytime." (145)
- Replace the glossary FAQ with buyer FAQs. Move "What does SDR stand for" to /tactics/sdr-meaning-in-sales/.
- Add the in-house vs outsourced table, the cost section, who it's for, results to expect, and the 3 SDR-program case studies (Clean Energy, Black River, Layfield).
- Make it the hub: link down to appointment setting, lead gen, calling, email and data.

**Appointment Setting (fix now, then expand)**
- **Delete the "Fractional / Full SDR" pricing table and the "$12,000 per month" FAQ answer.** Replace them with the shared /pricing block.
- Fix the meta (15+ → since 2007 / 19 years), remove the lowercase `product` schema, and fix Bid Retriever "1100" → 1,164.
- Add a qualified-meeting definition, show-rate and no-show policy, results to expect, and a C-level section linking to the new /tactics/c-level-appointment-setting/ guide.

**Lead Generation (protect, then improve carefully: 21% of site traffic)**
- Keep the URL and "B2B lead generation services" in the title and H1. Merge to **one H1**: "B2B Lead Generation Services". Add the brand to the title: `B2B Lead Generation Services | SalesRoads`. Trim the meta to ≤155 characters.
- Standardize on "Market Research Lead Generation" (not "Marketing Research").
- Add an FAQ and cost section. The SQL vs MQL comparison table is good; keep it.
- Make changes in one pass, then watch rankings for 2–3 weeks before touching it again.

**Cold Calling (expand)**
- One H1: "B2B Cold Calling Services". Remove "TDM's" and "17+ years".
- Add calling metrics (dials → conversations → meetings), compliance (TCPA/DNC), voicemail and callback strategy, US-based reps, and calling proof (Revel, Unishippers, Bid Retriever). Add an FAQ.

**Outbound Email (rebuild or fold in)**
- If it's not sold standalone: retitle as "Outbound Email Prospecting (Included in Every Program)" and keep it as a capability page linking to the program.
- Fix "Unbounce" and "dedicated IPs", the duplicate H1, the copied headings, the title and the meta. Keep the sample dashboard (5,214 contacted / 3.78% reply rate). It's the only page with real channel metrics.

**List Building + ABM Data + Intent Data (consolidate)**
- If not sold separately, merge ABM data and intent data into list building (301 both) and make that page "B2B List Building & Data". Keep the strong AI ICP validation and suppression copy, and add the 98% accuracy guarantee, the 500 records per SDR per month and 14+ sources from /pricing, plus one data-quality proof point.

**Inbound Appointment Setting / Account Reactivation (polish)**
- Fix the duplicate H1s and title suffixes. State clearly that they're **add-ons** scoped with a strategist, and say what they cost or how they're priced. Add a short FAQ. Their proof sections (Protecht, CSC) are good.
